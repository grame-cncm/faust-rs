## FAD: scope the memo cache per recursion binder

Commit date: 2026-09-05

Source commit: this commit

Found while writing the optimizers tutorial: the smallest in-graph learning
loop, `loop(g) = g - lr * (fad(loss(g), g) : !, _)` with
`loss(g) = (g * x - t)^2`, gave a gradient different from the hand-written
`2 (g x - t) x` — twice as large on the second frame, an RMS gap of 0.056
over 1000 frames — whenever `x` was itself a recursion (`no.noise`, an LCG,
a sawtooth), and the exact gradient when `x` was an audio input or the seed
was written `clip(lo, hi, g)`, which is what every `optimizers.lib` loop
does. `fad_recursive_local_projection.dsp`, the reference fixture for the
pattern, is only compile-checked, and the recursive runtime tests
differentiate with respect to controls, so nothing caught it.

Cause, read off `--dump-sig-dag`: the seed `g` is the recursive input,
`delay1(proj(0, ref(1)))` in de Bruijn form, and the back-edge of a
single-slot noise recursion is spelled exactly the same inside its own body.
`ForwardADTransform` memoizes by `SigId`, and the memo was not scoped: with
`g` visited before `x` (`g * x`), the seed's dual — tangent `1.0` — was
cached at the outer scope and then served *inside* the noise body, so the
LCG state received a tangent of `1103515245`, `x` a tangent of ~0.5, and the
loss derivative an extra `(g - 0.7) dx` term. The existing guard only
covered the opposite direction (body entries discarded on exit, so they
cannot shadow the outer seed check), and its unit test visits the inner
recursion *before* the seed, which is why it passed.

Fix in `forward_ad.rs`, `(Rec)` arm: on entering a body, stash the outer
cache and start from its closed entries only (`de_bruijn_aperture == 0`,
memoized on the transformer — closed terms mean the same thing at every
depth); restore the outer cache on exit, which also drops the body entries
as before. Two tests pin the two directions at the arena level
(`outer_seed_does_not_leak_into_an_inner_rec_body` alongside the existing
`fad_seed_not_poisoned_by_inner_rec_back_edge`), and
`recursive_projection_seed_stays_a_leaf_inside_an_unrelated_recursion` in
`fad_recursive_runtime.rs` compares the `fad` gradient with the hand-written
one frame by frame through the interpreter (it failed before the fix at
frame 0: `2.9e-6` against `-4.6e-11`). Golden snapshots are unchanged: no
tracked fixture reads a recursion from a loss seeded on a bare recursive
projection. `docs/fad-debruijn-recursion-en.md` §6.5 and §9 describe the
scoped cache.

After the fix, `faustprobe` reports a zero difference lane for the three
excitation shapes that used to diverge, and the optimizers fixtures are
unchanged (their seeds were never bare projections).

## optimizers.lib 0.5.0: loss-first loops, normalized and second-order engines, fixtures and tests

Commit date: 2026-09-05

Source commit: this commit

`libraries/optimizers.lib` was a FAD helper file with five copy-pasted loops
and five engines, an implicit instantaneous-MSE loss, an Adam without bias
correction (first steps `sqrt(1 - b2) / (1 - b1) = 3.16` times `lr`),
rectangular pole bounds that admit unstable filters, parameters that could
only start at `clip(0)`, and an `import("stdfaust.lib")` at the top. That
import resolves through `/usr/local/share/faust` (`compiler/src/paths.rs`),
which the tests may not depend on (AGENTS.md §3), so nothing in the workspace
exercised the library: no fixture, no test, no golden.

Version 0.5.0, documented to the Faust libraries convention
(<https://faustlibraries.grame.fr/contributing/>: `declare` header, section
banners, one block per public function with `Usage`/`Where`/`Test`/
`References`), prefix `op`, no import at all:

- **Loss-first loops** `descend_1D..5D(loss, engines..., bounds..., inits...,
  reset)`: one `fad` call over a scalar loss closed over the data, engines see
  `g = d(loss)/dp`. Losses `mse`, `pseudo_huber`, `logcosh`, `energy_loss`,
  `log_energy_loss`, regularizers `l2`, `l1s` — all smooth, since the `abs`
  rule is `NaN` at zero.
- **Least-squares loops** `lsq_1D..5D` (the old `optimize_ND` plus `init` and
  `reset`; `optimize_ND` kept as wrappers) with engines `lms`, `nlms`
  (sensitivity-normalized), `gn1`, and the original `sgd`/`adam`/`rmsprop`/
  `nadam`/`sign_sgd`. `adam` and `nadam` now bias-correct through
  `ema_bc(a, x) = ema(a, x) / ema(a, 1.0)` (`1.0 : smooth(a)` is exactly
  `1 - a^(n+1)`).
- **Gradient engines** `sgd_g`, `momentum_g`, `nesterov_g`, `adam_g`,
  `nadam_g`, `amsgrad_g`, `adabelief_g`, `rmsprop_g`, `adagrad_g`, `lion_g`,
  `sign_g`; conditioning `clip_g`, `softclip_g`, `gate_g`; schedules `lr_exp`,
  `lr_cos`, `warmup` (a learning rate is a signal); `polyak` readout.
- **Damped recursive Gauss-Newton** `lm_2D`, `lm_3D`: information matrix
  averaged with forgetting factor (bias corrected), Marquardt damping relative
  to its diagonal (unit-free), instantaneous innovation, gain `mu ~ 1 - a`.
  The averaged-innovation form was tried first and diverges — the same
  correction gets applied once per sample over the whole window.
- **Reparameterizations** `poles_from_reflection` (`a1 = k1 (1 + k2)`,
  `a2 = k2`: a bijection onto the stability triangle, so rectangular bounds
  on `k` are always stable), `reflection_from_poles`, `sigmoid_map`.
- **Newton solver** `newton_step`, `newton(N, F, y0)`, `F` and `F'` from one
  `fad`.

Measured with `faustprobe` before the change of API was settled (double
precision, `-I libraries`): resonant-filter identification with `lm_2D`
reaches `(1200.000000, 2.000000)` from `(1000, 1)` in 10 000 frames with a
single gain, where the per-parameter RMSProp of the synthesis note needs
`lr_f = 2.0`, `lr_q = 0.01`; NLMS on a 3-tap FIR converges at input levels
0.1, 1 and 10 alike while LMS is 100× too slow at 0.1 and clamps at 10;
`logcosh` keeps a gain within 1e-2 under ±20 spikes every 97 samples where
`mse` jitters by 0.2; the five-coefficient biquad started at `(a1, a2) =
(1.9, -0.5)` — inside the rectangle, outside the triangle — diverges to
`inf`, the reflection form converges from any start.

Tests. Seven fixtures `tests/corpus/opt_*.dsp` (LCG noise inline, stereo
residual output, `op = library("optimizers.lib")`, no standard library):
Adam gain, NLMS FIR at level 10, `lm_2D` pole + gain, `descend_5D` + Lion +
reflection biquad, `logcosh` under outliers, Newton cubic, and
`opt_all_functions.dsp`, generated from the `#### Test` entry of all 60
documented functions (95 outputs) so the documentation examples are compiled.
`crates/compiler/tests/optimizers_lib.rs` runs them through the interp fast
lane with `tests/corpus` and `libraries` as the only import paths and
requires the residual RMS to fall by 10× to 100× (f32 thresholds checked
against `faustprobe` runs in both precisions). `xtask golden`'s
`is_rust_golden_eligible` now skips any fixture naming a project-local
library (`interleave.lib`, `optimizers.lib`), with a unit test; the golden
search path does not contain `libraries`, and the runtime tests are the
coverage for these fixtures.

`docs/fad-rad-synthesis-{en,fr}.md` §4 now shows the biquad as
`descend_5D` + `lion_g` on an `lr_exp` schedule with the poles learned as
reflection coefficients (the code blocks of both versions were concatenated
and rendered: five coefficients within 1e-5 of the target at 300 000
frames). The previous `optimize_5D` + `rmsprop` form still compiles and
converges unchanged against 0.5.0 (bit-identical residual RMS 4.17444e-4 at
200 000 frames).

## Pin fmod's real nature against the C++ `%`/`fmod` conflation (f81491f8)

Commit date: 2026-09-05

Source commit: this commit

C++ Faust commit `f81491f84d92` (2026-09-04) fixed `fmod(int, int)` being
typed int: `SignalDispatch::xtendedOp` routed `XOp::Fmod` to the `Mod`
operation the `%` binop uses, whose nature rule is `x | y`. The WASM backend
then emitted `call $fmodf` on i32 operands, an invalid module that broke
every soundfile player (`so.loop`, `it.raise_modulo`); C++ hid it behind
implicit conversions, which is why the corpus stayed green.

Audit of faust-rs first. The bug cannot happen here, for two independent
reasons: `sigtype` has a dedicated `SigMatch::Fmod` rule with an explicit
`float_cast` (there is no shared operation to disambiguate), and the
promotion pass casts both `fmod` operands to real by primitive
(`promote_real_binary`), independently of the node's nature, after which the
retype makes the nature real anyway. Verified by mutation: with the
`float_cast` removed from the typing rule, FIR, C++ and WASM output were
unchanged and the `.wasm` modules still validated. The FIR checker would
NOT have caught it as an error had it happened: `FIR-FC03` treats all
numeric types as mutually compatible, `FIR-MA03` (integer-like argument to a
float math op) is a warning, and strict mode already fails on the ubiquitous
`FIR-M07` warnings, so it is not a usable gate. The WASM backend lowers
imported-call arguments without coercion, exactly like C++.

Two holes closed:

- `sigtype`: no unit test covered fmod's nature. Four added in `rules.rs`:
  `fmod(int, int)` is real, `remainder(int, int)` is real, `%` on two
  integers stays int, and the `so.loop` shape (int-seeded recursion wrapped
  by `fmod`) converges to real.
- `signal_prepare`: two tests in `tests/typing.rs` check that both `fmod`
  operands come out real after preparation with an explicit `FloatCast` on
  the `int()` operand, including inside a recursion body; and
  `verify_promotion_invariant` gains a clause refusing a prepared `fmod`,
  `remainder` or `atan2` whose result or operands are not real. This checker
  runs in production (`prepare_signals_for_fir_verified_with_options`).
- `tests/impulse-tests/dsp/math.dsp` receives the two lines from the C++
  commit (`fmod(int(_*9.2), 2.0)`, `fmod(int(_*9.2), int(2))`) and is again
  identical to the C++ file; `reference/math.ir` regenerated with the
  installed C++ Faust 2.87.11 (built after the fix), 76 → 78 outputs.

Mutation validation, per the plan/producer/checker methodology: removing the
`float_cast` from the `sigtype` rule fails exactly the two `fmod` tests;
replacing `promote_real_binary` by a neutral promotion for `fmod` fails the
two `signal_prepare` tests with the new invariant's message (`operands
Some(Int), Some(Int)`). Both mutations reverted.

Gates: `sigtype` 53/53, `transform --lib` 406/406, `compiler` 647/647;
`math.dsp` passes on all nine impulse lanes (cpp, c, interp, cranelift, wasm,
assemblyscript, rust, julia, cmajor); the 161 DSPs of `dsp/` + `dsp-rad/`
all compile through the widened invariant. Side note: the C++ oracle
preflight now classifies 39 DSPs as unsupported by the installed 2.87.11
(`ondemand`, `upsampling`, `downsampling`), the expected faust-rs-only
primitives, unrelated to this change.
