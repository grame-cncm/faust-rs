## fad around a held block: the lane the seed does not reach

Commit date: 2026-09-18

Source commit: this commit

The first use of the morning's change outside faust-diff-rir: in
faust-diff-fdn, the variant that holds the coefficients of the network by
`op.on_change` *while learning* (the loss's model takes the held
coefficients, so the `fad` tangents cross the block; `FDN_HOIST_LEARN=1` of
`scripts/gen_online.py` there). It refused to compile: `FRS-SFIR-0007 clocked
wrapper output is not a PermVar hold`, on every backend. Reproducer,
`fad(((_ != 0), _) : ondemand(_ <: (*(g), *(0.7))), g)`: a held lane the seed
does not reach. Its tangent is a literal zero (`depends_on_seed` false, the
subtree kept as it is), and `augment_block` (`forward_ad.rs`) put that
literal in the augmented payload next to the holds, where the lowering takes
every lane after the clock for a `PermVar` hold. In fdn the unreached lanes
were the cosines and sines of the angles, which the thirteen-lane program
does not learn; in rir every held coefficient depends on a learned
parameter, so the morning's measurements never met the case.

The zero tangent is not a held output: the block holds nothing for it and
its `Seq` consumers read the literal, which `Seq(od, y)` lowers as it is. So
`augment_block` leaves a literal tangent out of the payload. Test in
`crates/compiler/tests/ondemand_pipeline.rs` (the unreached lane's primal is
held, its tangent is zero, the reached lane's tangent is the held input); the
reverting mutation fails it. Gates as this morning, all green; golden
unchanged.

What the variant gives in faust-diff-fdn: it compiles in 5.1 s like the
per-sample program, its thirteen parameter lanes and its loss are the
per-sample program's to the last bit over twenty periods on a white target
(`--compare`, largest difference 1.9e-16 relative, one rounding), and it is
not faster while learning: 8.4× real time against 9.4× and 6.2× in two runs
of the per-sample program, the spread of the machine. The per-sample cost of
the learning program is the network's thirteen tangents through six lines
and their shelves, not the transcendental arithmetic of the coefficients,
which the block removes. The variant is kept as an option of the generator
and recorded in that repository's README.

## fad through an ondemand block: the memo admits clocked wrappers, and a constant of the fire block

Commit date: 2026-09-18

Source commit: this commit

Asked as "work on *fad through an ondemand block*", the constraint the
faust-diff-rir texts had recorded this morning: the online program with its
21 tangents compiled in 23 s with the coefficients taken as they are
(`rr.direct`) and in more than twelve minutes, 5.8 GB resident, with them held
by `op.on_change` (an `ondemand` fired when a parameter changes), so the
program took `direct`. Two things, one of them the cost, the other a bug found
on the way.

**The cost.** Reproducers first: without an FDN or without the EDR loss the
blow-up does not show; the smallest case is `rr.network` on 2 lines with 2
sections under `rr.edr_loss` and one `fad` tangent, and `--dump-sig-dag`
counts 4 159 propagated nodes with `direct` against 141 050 with `on_change`
(19 653 without the `fad`, 7 993 with one `frame_sum` block instead of the
loss's twenty). The cause is in propagation, not in the AD. The propagation
result memo (`crates/propagate/src/result_memo.rs`) refused its whole root as
soon as the program mentioned a clocked wrapper, because a wrapper allocates
a clock domain per propagation and "that delta has no replay protocol yet".
So a box shared between the twenty `frame_sum` ondemand blocks of the EDR
loss, here the network `h`, was re-propagated once per reference, and the
`fad` augmentation ran on each copy. C++ has no such gate: `makeClockEnv`
names a domain by the tuple `(parent, slotenv, path, box, inputs)`, which is
what the memo key holds (the parent domain through the mode component), so
the same wrapper box reached again in the same context with the same inputs
*is* the same block. The gate now admits the wrappers; a hit replays the
outputs of the first propagation and with them the domain they carry. The
table's doc said "one fresh domain per propagation"; it says "per miss" now,
and what the side table still refuses is the structural collision of two
instances in different contexts (the C++ de Bruijn class), which the key
keeps apart. Test: `par(i, 600, g)` on a shared `g = ondemand(...)` counts
fewer than 600 domains once the memo is warm (1 024 eligible calls), and the
same `g` under two different inputs still counts two; the reverting mutation
(the gate refusing wrappers again) gives 600. Measured: the smallest case
141 050 nodes to 6 088; the N=4, B=4, P=4 case 12.5 s to 0.42 s of
compilation (propagation 1.38 s to 0.038 s); the real online program with
`rr.direct` replaced by `op.on_change`, 17 min 29 s to 38.6 s (23 s on
`direct`); with the second change below it is 28.2 s, and the run is 0.35×
real time against 0.38× on `direct` (`--time`, the same command as
`prototype/checks.sh`), where a run before that change measured 0.16×.

**The bug.** With the memo fixed, the real program's `on_change` variant hit
`FRS-FIR-0001 use of undeclared variable 'fConst0'` on every backend (Cranelift
says "load of unknown variable"). Minimal reproducer, no `fad` needed:
`(_, 1.0) : ondemand(\(a, b).(a / ma.SR, b / ma.SR))(button("c"))`. The C
showed `float fConst0 = ...` local to `instanceConstants()` and, in
`compute()`'s fire block, `dsp->fPerm1 = (1.0f / fConst0)`. The held value
`1.0 / SR` is `Konst`, every parent edge it has in the signal DAG is `Konst`
too, so the placement analysis (`signal_fir/placement.rs`) saw no escape and
kept `SR`'s clamp on the init stack; but a `Clocked(env, y)` annotation means
`y` is emitted inside the guarded block, at fire time, whatever its
variability (C++ `generatePermVar` does the same). The analysis now hands
`Samp` down as the parent variability of a `Clocked` payload: a `Konst`
payload is hoisted into a struct constant and read from the block, its own
operands stay where they were. Test in
`crates/compiler/tests/clocked_emission_structure.rs` through the FIR
verifier (an undeclared variable is an error whether or not warnings are
fatal); the reverting mutation fails it. One thing seen and left: the
reciprocal `1/SR` shared by the two outputs is materialized twice, `fConst1`
and `fConst2` copies of one stack `fConst3`, the lowering cache being scoped
by region; cosmetic.

**Gates.** propagate, transform, compiler, codegen, cranelift-ffi and the
impulse runner green; `golden-check` unchanged on its 205 cases (the
placement rule changes no golden: none holds a `Konst` payload);
`code-graphs --check`, `structure-check`, clippy `-D warnings`, fmt. The
faust-diff-rir texts that recorded the constraint (the header of `rir.lib`,
`prototype/README.md`, ANALYSE.md §11) are corrected in that repository:
`op.on_change` is affordable now, and the choice of `direct` for the online
program is a choice again, not a constraint.

## A syntax error's repair suggestions, in one order at every parse

Commit date: 2026-09-18

Source commit: this commit

Found by the output harness of the faustprobe restructuring disagreeing with
itself on the one binary: the numbered list a syntax error prints (`1: Insert
INT`, `2: Insert CUT`, ...; some sixty lines for `bad = 1 +;`) came in another
order at every run, in `faustprobe` and in `faust-rs` alike. A numbered list
whose numbers change between runs cannot be referred to, and no byte-for-byte
comparison of diagnostics survives it.

The cause is in lrpar 0.13.10, not in this repository: `cpctplus::
simplify_repairs` deduplicates the repair sequences through a `HashSet`, whose
iteration order changes with every instance, then sorts them by length alone
with `sort_unstable_by`; its own comment says "amongst ParseRepairs of the
same rank, the ordering is non-deterministic". The grammar declares no
`%avoid_insert`, so length was the only criterion. `LexParseError::pp` then
printed them as they came.

The fix renders the message here (`parser::render_lex_parse_error`), in the
text lrpar writes (numbers right-aligned by its padding rule, consecutive
deletions merged, `\n` escaped), with the sequences ordered by the number of
edits they make, then by their text: a total order, the cheapest repairs first
as before, and the ties alphabetical. Checked against the old binary on four
kinds of error: the sets of rendered sequences and the padding are the same,
only the order differs. The documented examples (one or two repairs, already
alphabetical) stand.

Test: `crates/parser/tests/parse_error_order.rs`, three tests. The first
parses the same broken sources sixteen times in one process (each parse builds
a fresh recoverer, and a fresh hasher with it) and wants one text: it fails on
the old code. The second wants a dearer repair never before a cheaper one, on
`process = (1 2;`, whose 47 repairs mix two and three edits; a first version on
a source whose repairs all cost one survived the mutation "sort by text alone",
and was moved to this source. The third pins the exact text of a one-repair
error, the padding at 9 and 10, and the wording of a deletion. Three mutations
rejected: no sort (3 tests), sort by text alone (1), padding off by one (1).

`cargo test -p parser -p compiler -p cranelift-ffi` 1173 pass; fmt, clippy,
`golden-check` and `code-graphs --check` pass.
