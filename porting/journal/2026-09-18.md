Asked what to think of pull request 1293 of `grame-cncm/faust` (benchmark
framework, Mojo backend with explicit SIMD, experimental GPU) if its ideas
were taken into faust-rs, then to detail "the GPU on another parallel
axis", then how the classical DDSP systems occupy a GPU and how near
faust-rs can get. Written as
`porting/mojo-gpu-pr-1293-analysis-and-batch-axis-2026-09-18-en.md`, from
the pull request's description, its three READMEs, `mojo_instructions_gpu.cpp`
and its benchmark plot; nothing run. What the plot says, re-read after a
first version overstated the parity: Mojo scalar against C++ scalar from

±25 %; where vectorization pays, on the programs that cost most per
sample, both `-vec` series gain ×1.7 to ×1.9, the explicit SIMD doing what
clang's autovectorizer does; where it does not, the C++ `-vec -vs 4` is
slower than scalar by 20 to 70 %, `-vs 4` being what the Mojo backend
imposes. The SIMD emission exists because Mojo disables LLVM
autovectorization, and it rests on couplings by name (`vindex`, bargraph
fields), a "last store decides" assumption, one validated precision pair
(`-double` with float buffers, the ordinary deployment pair, not the
`FAUSTFLOAT double` of the impulse tests) and no tail loop. The GPU
path puts one device thread per sub-loop of the `-vec` DAG, one kernel per
stage and per chunk of `-vs` frames, each thread sequential in time: the
task parallelism of `-omp`/`-sch` on an accelerator, with no measurement
given and none to expect at that grain. The axis that fits is the
instance: N copies of one program with their own state, which the
lockstep bundle already certifies, and which `multistart`, the ES, the
`faustprobe` grids and an offline dataset already are; the lane becomes
the thread, one launch per block, the pull request's architecture layer
(device copy, control map, adapter) reusable, its visitor not. Against the
DDSP systems, axis by axis: the batch reachable, the non-recursive
sub-loops already classified, the linear recursions detectable by the
FIR/IIR reveal where a PyTorch user chooses the frequency-sampling form by
hand, the nonlinear ones sequential for everyone, frames and matrices out
of reach; forward mode is not a handicap below a few dozen parameters.
Completed the same day after a second look: the batch axis needs no
compiler change to exist, N instance structures side by side in the
architecture and a device-compatible scalar emitter suffice, the
lockstep split buying only coalescing and the CPU widening; the instance
axis has real-time uses too, voices and channels, with the block size
bounded by the transfer; the non-recursive sub-loops give a second grid
dimension, and a recursion through a delay of D samples is D independent
chains; FFT evaluation of a revealed IIR is an approximation with the
state to carry, the scan of a time-varying linear recursion is exact in
another summation order; the autograd bridge has two shapes, `fad` when
the Faust block is fed by a recording, `rad` when a network feeds it and
dL/dx is needed; and what GEMM is, why the GPU is built around it, and why
Faust unrolls a dense layer instead of producing one. What is worth
taking, in order: an `xtask bench` over backends × modes, built on the
impulse runner, with `-vs` swept; explicit SIMD FIR nodes for Cranelift
and WASM, which have no autovectorizer, making the lockstep independent
of clang and the contraction policy explicit; the batch axis by the
architecture first; the Mojo backend itself not now.

## `iterate` as the general wrapper, and a false sentence in the ondemand note

Commit date: 2026-09-18

Source commit: this commit

Asked whether `iterate`, as a primitive, would be more general than the
three wrappers, and whether they could be built on it. Section 2.7 of the
plan answers with measurements: `ondemand` in both readings is `iterate`
with a flag constantly 1, since the clock is cast to int by the normal form
and the body runs `⌊H⌋` times, the `if` of a [0, 1] range being the
emission of `⌊H⌋ ∈ {0, 1}`; `downsampling(3)` is identical sample for
sample to `ondemand` with a divided clock computed in the parent domain;
`upsampling(3)` is identical to integer `ondemand` whose body zero-stuffs
its input with the in-sample index. What no wrapper written in the library
can do is the sample rate seen inside (`SR * H`, `SR / H`), a property of
the clock environment set in `make_clock_env`: the layering is one loop
primitive, the zero-stuffing as a body rewrite, the rate as a domain
property, the three keywords as derived forms.

On the way, `docs/ondemand-note-{en,fr}.md` said that a real clock of 0.5
with a [0, 1] range runs the body once, "since 0.5 ≠ 0". It does not:
`faust-rs` emits `int iSlow0 = (int)(fHslider0)` and tests it, so 0.5 is 0
and the body never runs, and a clock of 2.5 runs it twice; the C++
reference branch (`8eebea429`, `faust --version` 2.84.3) emits the same
cast and the same test. The paragraph is corrected in both languages. The
refusal of a "real-valued non-boolean clock" in `select_guard_shape` is
unreachable after that cast; left as it is, noted in the plan.

## `for` or `while`: the plan for a counted clock loop with an exit flag

Commit date: 2026-09-18

Source commit: this commit

Asked whether emitting a `while` rather than a `for` for an integer clock
would be more flexible or more powerful, after the solver of the overview's
section 2.5. The answer, then the plan:
`porting/repeat-counted-loop-with-exit-analysis-and-plan-2026-09-18-en.md`.
The difference is where the exit is evaluated, outside before the loop
(the clock, an outer signal) or inside on the iterate; not more powerful,
because real time needs the bound anyway and `while (cond && n < Kmax)` is
the counted loop with an early exit, which the two-layer form of the
overview already computes; more flexible, because the exit reads the
iterate, one layer replaces three, and the condition can be anything
computed inside (an adaptive integrator's sub-stepping). Measured what the
two-layer form wastes: the unused iterations of a budget of 8 against 3
cost 4 to 12 % of the solver on a 100 Hz sine, in the spread of the
measurement; and a budget too small is wrong, not slow (white noise,
budget 3: unconverged samples at 1e-8).

The form proposed is a fourth wrapper, `iterate(C)`: `C : u → v+1`,
`iterate(C) : u+1 → v`, the last output of the body a continue flag
consumed by the primitive, the clock still the bound, the FIR a
`WhileLoop` on `(lOd < H) & (iStop == 0)` (the FIR has no `break`; the
`WhileLoop` node exists with every emitter but the interpreter's, and
nothing produces one today). A new signal tag `SIGITER` rather than a
convention on `SIGOD`'s last lane, the flag a `Clocked` payload without a
`PermVar`; the `fad` augmentation gives it a primal only. Not for
`upsampling`, whose input arrives on the last iteration. Some forty files
in the surface, most of them one arm; five phases with the checks written
first and a mutation each; four decisions open, the keyword and the flag
polarity first.

## fad through an ondemand block: the memo admits clocked wrappers, and a constant of the fire block

Commit date: 2026-09-18

Source commit: this commit

Asked as "work on *fad through an ondemand block*", the constraint the
faust-diff-rir texts had recorded this morning: the online program with its
21 tangents compiled in 23 s with the coefficients taken as they are
(`rr.direct`) and in more than twelve minutes, 5.8 GB resident, with them held
by `op.on_change` (an `ondemand` fired when a parameter changes), so the
program took `direct`. Two things, one of them the cost, the other a bug found
on the way.

**The cost.** Reproducers first: without an FDN or without the EDR loss the
blow-up does not show; the smallest case is `rr.network` on 2 lines with 2
sections under `rr.edr_loss` and one `fad` tangent, and `--dump-sig-dag`
counts 4 159 propagated nodes with `direct` against 141 050 with `on_change`
(19 653 without the `fad`, 7 993 with one `frame_sum` block instead of the
loss's twenty). The cause is in propagation, not in the AD. The propagation
result memo (`crates/propagate/src/result_memo.rs`) refused its whole root as
soon as the program mentioned a clocked wrapper, because a wrapper allocates
a clock domain per propagation and "that delta has no replay protocol yet".
So a box shared between the twenty `frame_sum` ondemand blocks of the EDR
loss, here the network `h`, was re-propagated once per reference, and the
`fad` augmentation ran on each copy. C++ has no such gate: `makeClockEnv`
names a domain by the tuple `(parent, slotenv, path, box, inputs)`, which is
what the memo key holds (the parent domain through the mode component), so
the same wrapper box reached again in the same context with the same inputs
*is* the same block. The gate now admits the wrappers; a hit replays the
outputs of the first propagation and with them the domain they carry. The
table's doc said "one fresh domain per propagation"; it says "per miss" now,
and what the side table still refuses is the structural collision of two
instances in different contexts (the C++ de Bruijn class), which the key
keeps apart. Test: `par(i, 600, g)` on a shared `g = ondemand(...)` counts
fewer than 600 domains once the memo is warm (1 024 eligible calls), and the
same `g` under two different inputs still counts two; the reverting mutation
(the gate refusing wrappers again) gives 600. Measured: the smallest case
141 050 nodes to 6 088; the N=4, B=4, P=4 case 12.5 s to 0.42 s of
compilation (propagation 1.38 s to 0.038 s); the real online program with
`rr.direct` replaced by `op.on_change`, 17 min 29 s to 38.6 s (23 s on
`direct`); with the second change below it is 28.2 s, and the run is 0.35×
real time against 0.38× on `direct` (`--time`, the same command as
`prototype/checks.sh`), where a run before that change measured 0.16×.

**The bug.** With the memo fixed, the real program's `on_change` variant hit
`FRS-FIR-0001 use of undeclared variable 'fConst0'` on every backend (Cranelift
says "load of unknown variable"). Minimal reproducer, no `fad` needed:
`(_, 1.0) : ondemand(\(a, b).(a / ma.SR, b / ma.SR))(button("c"))`. The C
showed `float fConst0 = ...` local to `instanceConstants()` and, in
`compute()`'s fire block, `dsp->fPerm1 = (1.0f / fConst0)`. The held value
`1.0 / SR` is `Konst`, every parent edge it has in the signal DAG is `Konst`
too, so the placement analysis (`signal_fir/placement.rs`) saw no escape and
kept `SR`'s clamp on the init stack; but a `Clocked(env, y)` annotation means
`y` is emitted inside the guarded block, at fire time, whatever its
variability (C++ `generatePermVar` does the same). The analysis now hands
`Samp` down as the parent variability of a `Clocked` payload: a `Konst`
payload is hoisted into a struct constant and read from the block, its own
operands stay where they were. Test in
`crates/compiler/tests/clocked_emission_structure.rs` through the FIR
verifier (an undeclared variable is an error whether or not warnings are
fatal); the reverting mutation fails it. One thing seen and left: the
reciprocal `1/SR` shared by the two outputs is materialized twice, `fConst1`
and `fConst2` copies of one stack `fConst3`, the lowering cache being scoped
by region; cosmetic.

**Gates.** propagate, transform, compiler, codegen, cranelift-ffi and the
impulse runner green; `golden-check` unchanged on its 205 cases (the
placement rule changes no golden: none holds a `Konst` payload);
`code-graphs --check`, `structure-check`, clippy `-D warnings`, fmt. The
faust-diff-rir texts that recorded the constraint (the header of `rir.lib`,
`prototype/README.md`, ANALYSE.md §11) are corrected in that repository:
`op.on_change` is affordable now, and the choice of `direct` for the online
program is a choice again, not a constraint.

## A syntax error's repair suggestions, in one order at every parse

Commit date: 2026-09-18

The cause is in lrpar 0.13.10, not in this repository: `cpctplus::
simplify_repairs` deduplicates the repair sequences through a `HashSet`, whose

text lrpar writes (numbers right-aligned by its padding rule, consecutive
deletions merged, `\n` escaped), with the sequences ordered by the number of
edits they make, then by their text: a total order, the cheapest repairs first
as before, and the ties alphabetical. Checked against the old binary on four
kinds of error: the sets of rendered sequences and the padding are the same,
only the order differs. The documented examples (one or two repairs, already
alphabetical) stand.
