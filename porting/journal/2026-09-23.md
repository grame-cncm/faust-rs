## Effect orientation cubic in the number of foreign calls, made incremental

Commit date: 2026-09-23

Source commit: this commit

`rad(sum(k, K, ma.tanh((_ * g)@k)), g)` compiled in 24 ms at K = 40, 1.1 s
at 160, 15.7 s at 320 (the recursive variant 37 s), and the same program
without `rad` took the same time: the cost was not in the block reverse
sweep. Propagation and the prepared forest were instant; `sample` put every
cycle in `hgraph::orient_effect_conflicts`, called by `build_scalar_gate`,
inside `dependency_reachable`. Every `ffunction` carries
`ForeignPurity::Unknown` and is therefore a barrier ordered against every
stateful node, and each (barrier, node) pair was tested with two full walks
of the graph: K barriers × 2K stateful nodes × a graph of 5K nodes.

The barrier loop now builds, per barrier, the set of nodes it reaches and
the set of nodes that reach it (`extend_forward`, `extend_backward` over
predecessor lists), walked once and extended by the edges the loop adds: an
edge out of the barrier extends the first set from its target, an edge into
it extends the second from its source, exactly what a fresh walk would have
found at that step, since every edge goes from a later baseline position to
an earlier one. The edge set and its insertion order are unchanged, so every
schedule is: the seven reproducers' C++ is byte-identical, `golden-check`
passes, and `hgraph::tests::barrier_orientation_equals_the_pairwise_reference`
keeps the pairwise algorithm as the reference the incremental one must
equal edge for edge on a sum of twelve foreign calls on delayed reads.
`faustprobe --fd-check` on the K = 80 reproducer gives the same gradient
before and after (3.6e-13). Times: K = 320 from 13.7 s to 0.18 s, K = 640
1.05 s, 1280 7.3 s; what remains above 640 is the normal-form arithmetic of
a K-deep chain of additions (`normalize_add_term`, the gcd of its terms),
quadratic as in the reference compiler and independent of `rad` or effects;
a `par(...) :> _` sum does not pay it. `faust-diff-fdn/dsp/fdn_mezza.dsp`
with its full 321-sample echo-density window compiles in 0.8 s instead of
51 s. Whether a libm `ffunction` should be a barrier at all (the reference
treats them as pure expressions) is a separate question left open.

## How large a FIR or IIR compiles under fad and rad, measured

Commit date: 2026-09-23

Source commit: this commit

Section 14 of the two syntheses: `fi.fir` of 64 to 4096 taps, `fi.iir` of
order 16 to 256, cascades of 16 to 256 `fi.tf2`, the coefficients as slider
seeds, timed with `faustprobe --time` and checked with `--fd-check` on
lanes up to order 256. FIR is quadratic in both modes (each lane an N-tap
FIR): 1024 taps in 5 s, 4096 in five minutes and gigabytes. A recursion is
quadratic under `fad` (each tangent a full recursion: order 64 in minutes,
256 not compiled) and linear under `rad` (order 256 or 256 sections in
under 3 s, real time). Rule: `rad` for high-order recursions or more than a
hundred parameters. Compared with FLAMO's frequency sampling, whose FIR
and FDN gradients are products and a matrix inverse.
Then, against the DDSP literature (a subsection of the same section 14):
time-domain differentiable IIRs stay at orders 1 to 6 (Kuznetsov 2020,
Yu 2024, Yu and Fazekas 2025, whose C++ kernel at order 2 runs some 6000x
real time per pass on one core, naive autograd 1000x slower), our compiled
`rad` on a few biquads is in that class with every gradient lane emitted
at once; high-order IIRs and long FIRs only exist by frequency sampling
(DDSP, active acoustics with FIRs of order 1000 on 480 000 frequency
points, GRAFX, the Aalto FDNs). Our gaps: batching, a spectral loss, the
long FIR.

## The two examples of the AES 2025 paper in the corpus; a zero gradient through a wired recursion slot fixed

Commit date: 2026-09-23

Source commit: this commit

"Faust Autodiff: Towards Audio Domain-Specific Machine Learning" (T. Rushton,
AES AIMLA 2025) differentiates at the source level by pattern matching on the
box algebra; its neuron (listing 4) and its order-2 IIR (listing 5) are now
corpus fixtures with the paper's own routing and the parameters as slider
seeds: `fad_neuron_sigmoid`, `rad_neuron_sigmoid`, `fad_iir_transposed`,
`rad_iir_transposed`. `crates/compiler/tests/aes_autodiff_paper.rs` checks
the neuron against the closed form `y (1 - y) x_i` and `rad` against `fad`
lane by lane, the IIR's tangents against central finite differences on every
frame and against `fad(fi.iir(bv, av), seeds)` (identical to the bit, where
the paper cannot differentiate the library filter), and the IIR's `rad`
block totals against the finite differences of the block sum and the `fad`
totals. `faustprobe --fd-check` agrees to 1e-12 on the five coefficients.

The `rad` twin of the IIR found a defect. The paper's recursion,
`((sub, par(l, N, _)) ~ fir(N))`, routes the coefficients through the `~`
block as wires: the recursion has N + 1 slots, and the body reads each
coefficient only through its feedback tap `Delay1(Proj(slot, SYMREF))`, the
carrier never reads that slot outside. The block reverse sweep re-injected
the carry of a tap into the `Proj(slot, SYMREC)` node of the postorder for
that slot, and there was none: the carry was stored at each reverse step and
never loaded, the coefficient's gradient was zero while `fad` and the finite
difference agreed (`(+, _) ~ *` reproduces it with one slider). The
postorder is now closed over such slots (`collect_bra_postorder_closed` in
`crates/transform/src/signal_fir/block_reverse_ad.rs`, used by the sweep and
by the tape planning so both see the same graph): a tap whose `(var, slot)`
no `Proj(slot, SYMREC(var))` node covers has the slot's body walked, and the
sweep accumulates the carry into that body, which forwards it as the
projection would. A body walked this way lands after the nodes that reach
it only through the tap, so the reverse walk still meets its adjoint before
its children. Checked with `--fd-check` on the coefficient as a slider, as a
square of the seed, as an input-dependent signal, and through two nested
loops. `rad_runtime.rs` gains the one-slider case against finite
differences; `rad-note-en.md` §4 and §6, the two syntheses (§13).

Gates: fmt, clippy, the workspace tests, the xtask checks and
`golden-check`; `compile-budget-check` still cannot measure on this machine
(3 ms calibration, 4 ms floor).

## Dead widgets pruned from the interface, as the reference does

Commit date: 2026-09-23

Source commit: this commit

The reference compiler builds its interface while generating code
(`ScalarCompiler::generateHSlider` and its siblings call
`fUITree.addUIWidget`, `compiler/generator/compile_scal.cpp`; the tree is
sorted once at the end), so a widget whose signal the simplified graph no
longer reaches never appears: cut by `!`, absorbed by a folded zero, left in
the dead branch of a `select2` with a constant selector, a bargraph whose
output is dropped. faust-rs registered every widget of the box tree at
propagation (`ui_build.rs` walks the boxes, both sides of every
composition) and listed them all: `hslider(...), _ : !, _` showed a slider
the reference does not, `ampmodeler.dsp` 29 controls against 25.

The pruning sits in the fast lane, `compile_fastlane_inner`
(`crates/transform/src/signal_fir/mod.rs`), right after preparation, where
the final simplified DAG exists and before both module builders; every
consumer of the interface is downstream (the scalar and vector FIR, the
JSON built from the module, faustprobe's `--list-params` replayed from
`buildUserInterface`, box-ffi and signal-ffi). `live_controls` walks the
prepared outputs and collects the controls of every widget, bargraph and
soundfile node; `UiProgram::pruned` (`crates/ui/src/lib.rs`) rebuilds the
layout without the dead leaves, drops a group left empty, keeps the root
(an interface with no control is the root box opened and closed, as in the
reference), and promotes a lone surviving group to root when the root had
been synthesized over several items, as the builder would have done had the
dead items never existed. The registry and the ids embedded in the signals
are untouched, so every lookup by id is as before; `emit_ui_program` and
`build_vector_ui_fir` declare a zone for the controls the layout still shows
(`controls_in_tree`), so a dead widget gets neither a field nor a
`buildUserInterface` entry, as in the reference's generated code. A program
without dead widgets pays a walk of its signals and nothing else
(`pruned` returns `None`).

One family is kept whatever the signals read: the seeds of `fad` and
`rad`. A seed the body never reads has a zero tangent and no occurrence in
the final signals, yet a host trains or probes it by its path
(`faustprobe --train`, `--fd-check`), and the compiler's own contract says
it keeps its leaf (`ad_seed_the_body_never_reads_keeps_its_own_ui_leaf`);
propagation records them in `UiProgram::pinned`, which `pruned` honours. A
probe fixture that read three controls through `0 * (...)` now reads them
through `min(0, abs(...))`, which folds to nothing and keeps them alive; the
transform test that wanted a field for an unread slider asserts the
opposite, and a twin checks the pinned case.

What propagation returns (`SignalCompileOutput::ui`) stays whole: sigtype
and preparation need every control, and the tests of propagation count them
there. The duplicate-address check moved with the interface: it ran right
after propagation on every widget of the box tree, and refused a dead widget
at a live widget's address where the reference, which checks the paths it
writes to its JSON, accepts it. It now runs in the fast lane, after the
pruning, on the interface the module shows
(`SignalFirErrorCode::UiDuplicatePath`, the conflicts carried by the error),
and `transform_error_to_compiler` renders it as before,
`CompilerError::UiLayout` with every declaration labeled
(`ui_paths::ui_layout_error`). `compile_*_to_signals` no longer rejects a
duplicated address, the reference's signals do not either; any module or
JSON does. Fixture `ui_dead_08_duplicate_address_dead.dsp`, accepted by both
compilers with one control; the live pair still refused by both.

Fixtures `tests/corpus/ui_dead_01..07_*.dsp` (cut slider, folded zero,
constant `select2`, cut bargraph, a group emptied, `attach` keeping a widget,
nested groups emptied) with their goldens; `crates/compiler/tests/dead_widgets.rs`
freezes the reference's arity and interface (faust 2.88.1, `-json`) for the
seven, compares them live with the `faust` binary when installed, and checks
that the generated C++ of the cut slider has neither its field nor its
`addHorizontalSlider`. `modulation_11_two_input_drop` now expects the
reference's interface, its exception removed. `ampmodeler.dsp` lists 25
controls in both compilers.

## Widget modulation: three defects against the reference, 45 corpus fixtures

Commit date: 2026-09-23

Source commit: this commit

Widget modulation (`["label": m -> e]`) was implemented from the C++ source
but tested by three fixtures with the identity modulator and no reference
comparison. 44 programs covering the manual's rules and the edge cases were
rendered by faust 2.88.1 (impulse-test architecture, `-json`) and by
faust-rs (`faustprobe --protocol impulse-test`, `--json`): 38 equal to the
bit with the same interface, three defects, all in the normalisation of
labels, none in the algorithm.

- A widget whose label opens a group, `hslider("h:sub/x", …)`, was one
  segment `h:sub/x`: `"x"` and `"sub/x"` matched nothing. Now
  `widget_label_path_segments` (`crates/eval/src/label.rs`) decodes the label
  with the `ui` crate's `normalize_widget_label_path`, as the reference's
  `superNormalizePath` does through `label2path`: `["x", "sub"]`.
- Metadata was cut at the first `[`: a label starting with a declaration,
  `"[1] Wet [tooltip: …]"` (every `*_demo` slider), became empty, and the
  manual's own example `["Wet" -> dm.freeverb_demo]` failed on arity.
  `strip_label_metadata` is now the `ui` crate's `split_label_metadata`, the
  port of `extractMetadata` (every declaration removed, escapes, nesting,
  trimmed); `eval` depends on `ui`.
- A target matching nothing added no input, where the reference always
  wraps the slot of a two-input modulator (`boxSymbolic(slot, mbody)`), one
  input more, unconnected, and warns under `-wall`. faust-rs does the same:
  the slot is wrapped whether or not a widget matched, and the run records
  `EvalWarning::ModulationNoMatch` (`EvalStats::warnings`, collected on
  `LoopDetector`), which the compiler reports as `FRS-EVAL-0008` under the
  semantic warnings, tied to the node and the source like an eval error.

Two facts read off the reference and frozen in fixtures: a group prefix
written with its type (`"v:in/x"`, the manual's `h:group/label` form)
matches nothing in 2.88.1, `target2path` keeping `v:in` verbatim, where
`"in/x"` matches; a target naming only a group modulates every widget under
it with one shared slot. One divergence outside modulation, left as is: a
widget whose signal reaches nothing (`["a": (!, _) -> e]`, or plainly
`hslider(…) : !`) stays in faust-rs's interface where the reference drops it.

Fixtures: `tests/corpus/modulation_01..42_*.dsp`, self-contained (an inline
LFO and one-pole in place of `os.osc` and `si.smoo`, a stand-in for
`freeverb_demo` with its `[1] Wet [tooltip: …]` label), and
`err_27..29_modulation_*.dsp` (three inputs, two outputs, a constant on a
bargraph), with their goldens. `crates/compiler/tests/modulation_corpus.rs`
freezes the reference's arity and interface (paths in UI order, kinds,
init/min/max/step) for the 42, compares them live with the `faust` binary
when one is installed, checks the three refusals and the warning.
`crates/eval/tests/core_eval.rs`: the no-match test rewritten for the
dangling slot and the warning, one added for a one-input modulator.
`porting/control-inputs-and-wildcard-modulation-analysis-2026-09-22-en.md`
section 2.2 records the verification; `docs/diagnostics-codes-reference-en.md`
gains the code.

Validated with `cargo fmt --all`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --
-D warnings`, `cargo test --workspace --all-targets`, the five structure
gates and `golden-check`. `compile-budget-check` could not measure on this
machine, with or without the change: its calibration program compiles in
3 ms, under the 4 ms floor the ratio needs.

## Analysis revised: `cinputs` and `coutputs` return the widgets, not their count

Commit date: 2026-09-23

Source commit: this commit

`porting/control-inputs-and-wildcard-modulation-analysis-2026-09-22-en.md`,
section 3: `cinputs(e)` is now the list of the N widget boxes of `e`, in UI
order, the same nodes as in `e`, and `coutputs(e)` the list of its
bargraphs; the count is `outputs(cinputs(e))`, and the counting primitive
disappears. `cinput(i, e)` and `coutput(i, e)` keep their tuples
`(widget, init, min, max, step)` and `(bargraph, min, max)`. The seeds of a
descent become one identifier, `fad(op.mse(e, target), cinputs(e))`, and the
tuple's width stays inside `cinput`, the only place a sixth entry would
change. A list of tuples was considered and rejected (no list of lists in
Faust: ten boxes in parallel, a count divided by five). A program without
controls gives the empty box `0 : !`, which `adaptive_fad` must report
rather than fail on a `par` of zero. The implementation plan and the tests follow
the change. Section 3.4 now carries the envisaged code of `adaptive_fad` and
`adaptive_rad`, one helper `_adaptive(descend, e, loss, upd, clock, reset, X, t)`
called on `descend_N_fad_clocked` or `descend_N_rad_clocked`: the parameters
enter the wildcard-modulated `e` as its first N inputs, the loss is applied
after the model through a one-parameter lambda per output, and the audio
inputs and the target are arguments, closed over as signals, since Faust has
no lambda over a bus of N and `mse` mentions its argument twice. The first
sketch's `\(P).(loss(model(P), target))` was a box of one input whatever N.
