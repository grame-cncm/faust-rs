## `optimizers.lib` 0.9.0: initialisation and continuation helpers (plan phase N6)

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

First phase of `porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`.
Five public functions, each with its `Test` entry (`opt_all_functions.dsp`
now 85 entries, 144 outputs): `init_latch(T, e)` holds the value an estimate
has at sample `T`; `init_reset(T)` is 1 up to and including `T`, the
loop's `reset` while the estimate is observed (the plan's one-sample
`init_pulse` would have let the loop descend on a moving `init` before the
latch, so the held form replaced it); `ramp_lin` and `ramp_exp` are the
schedules as plain signals, to anneal a model parameter as well as a rate,
and `lr_exp` is now `ramp_exp` (bit-identical, asserted by
`opt_ramp_alias.dsp`); `stalled(a, eps_g, eps_l, g, l)` flags a small
gradient under a high loss (`opt_stalled_lanes.dsp`: 0, 1, 0 on a
descending, a stuck and a converged segment).

**The fixture and what it found.** `opt_init_latch_string.dsp` starts the
waveguide string from its own estimate of the target's pitch, the peak lag
of the smoothed autocorrelation over a 4-sample grid from 161 to 279 Hz
(the standard `an.pitchTracker`, zero-crossing based, reads hundreds of
hertz or single digits on this noise-driven string), frozen 2 % above the
target to land inside the capture zone of the ±1 Hz well: init frozen at
222.77 Hz, pitch 219.998 Hz at 24 000 samples, 220.000000 from 48 000 on,
residual under 1e-6, with no start chosen by hand. Writing it exposed a
compile-time trap: a recursion whose body closes over a large term
re-lowers it at every mention. An 8-lane estimate feeding `lsq_1D` took
34 s (4 s per further lane, 115 s at 24), 6 s for a hand-written loop with
no `fad`, 0.07 s for the same graph outside a recursion, and 0.27 s for a
`fad` loop whose body does not close over it; neither an `ondemand` wrapper
(3 s at 8 lanes, 6m47 at 148) nor a table between the two helped. The
three 1D loops (`lsq_1D`, `descend_1D`, `descend_1D_clocked`) now take
`init` through an input wire of their recursive block instead of closing
over it (0.46 s at 8 lanes, 1.5 s for the 30-lane fixture, 19 s in the
debug test build); the multi-parameter loops still close over theirs, the
overview's section 6 says so, and the compiler issue is filed as a task
with the repro. Same trajectories: the optimizers (17 → 18 tests), DDSP
(12) and tutorial (25) suites pass unchanged.

**Mutation gate.** A first version of the string test only checked the
lock, and `init_reset` never holding (`<= 0`) still passed it: the loop
converges even when it descends on the moving estimate before the latch.
The test now also reads the gap between the pitch lane and the init lane
over the observation. It is not zero even when held: the loops apply the
engine step to a zeroed deviation and report that, so the output sits one
step from `init` (mean gap 0.04 Hz, a 2.4 Hz transient when the estimate
jumps a lag), against 2.8 Hz in double and 4.65 Hz in the single-precision
test when the loop is not held; the threshold is 0.5 Hz. With it, the
never-hold mutation fails, `init_latch` freezing at sample 0 (before any
observation) fails on the frozen value (310 Hz, outside the capture zone),
and `stalled` returning 0 fails the lanes test on the stuck segment. All
three reverted.

**Docs.** Overview (both languages): version, section 3 table, section 4.5,
section 5 (three measured rows), section 6 (the closure trap), section 9
(what now exists, what still does not); the string example's "Try" line
points at the fixture; the plan carries N6's status and its two deviations.

## Plan: `optimizers.lib` beyond the gradient's basin

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

Follow-up of the non-convexity section: the overview now says what the
library does not offer when the start is in the wrong basin or the parameter
has no derivative. `porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`
plans six additions in the library's idiom, ordered N6 → N4 → N1 → N3 → N2 →
N5 by value, cost and dependency:

- **N6** initialisation and continuation helpers (`init_latch`, `init_pulse`,
  `ramp_lin`, `ramp_exp` with `lr_exp` kept as a bit-identical alias,
  `stalled`), on the state form `init + deviation` where `init` is a signal;
- **N4** `langevin_g`, a noisy engine with an annealed temperature;
- **N1** gradient-free clocked loops, `spsa_1D_clocked`, `search_1D_clocked`
  ((1+1)-ES) and `spsa_N_clocked`: two model evaluations per frame on the same
  excitation, no tangent, so an integer delay, a `select2` or a written table
  become learnable;
- **N3** `descend_1D_restart`, a sequential multi-start driven by a plateau
  detector;
- **N2** `multistart_1D` and `grid_then_descend_1D` on an `argmin_K` fold;
- **N5** basin-widening losses (`corr_loss`, `bank_log_energy_loss`,
  `frame_spectral_loss`), gated by a landscape scan of the string with
  `faustprobe --set` that must show a wider well before any learning.

Each phase names its fixtures, an independent check (SPSA estimate against
the `fad` gradient on a smooth loss; the fold against an argmin computed in
Rust; loss symmetry and floor; ramp alias identity) and a hand-applied
mutation the check must reject. The shared bed is the waveguide string, whose
landscape is the only one the repository has measured (±1 Hz well, drift from
200 and 176 Hz), plus an integer-delay variant on which `fad` gives a zero
tangent. Every number in the plan is a target to measure. Verified while
writing: `an.pitchTracker` exists in the installed faustlibraries; no planned
name collides with `optimizers.lib` 0.8.0. Nothing is implemented.

## `optimizers.lib` overview: a section on non-convexity

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

The word "convex" appeared nowhere in `docs/` or `libraries/`, although the
question decides whether a DDSP program learns: gradient descent guarantees
the global minimum only for a convex loss, and almost no loss in the
optimizers documentation is convex (resonator frequency and Q, biquad poles,
notch angle, FDN T60, string delay length, GRU and MLP weights), while nearly
all of them converge. The examples already carried the answer in measured
form: the waveguide string's ±1 Hz well on a flat plateau (228 → 220 Hz
converges, 200 → 190 drifts), the notch basin set by its radius, the
`log_energy_loss` that unsticks a cutoff `mse` leaves at the 20 Hz bound, the
damping annealed from 0.70 to 0.95.

**Docs.** New section 9 in `libraries/optimizers-overview-fr.md` and
`-en.md` (same content, same structure, 16 bold or bulleted leads each),
references renumbered to 10. It states what gradient descent actually asks
of the landscape (a starting point in the right basin, an informative
gradient, comparable scales), sorts the repository's examples into convex
and non-convex, walks through the measured string counter-example, maps
each library tool to one of the three questions (`init`, reparameterisation,
loss choice, continuation, `lm_2D`/`lm_3D`, and the `_rad` direct term that
changes nothing), and lists what the gradient cannot do alone (multi-start,
coarse grid then gradient, gradient-free search, discrete parameters),
saying explicitly that none of it is in the library and sketching the two
natural forms, in-graph and host-side, neither written nor measured. Every
number quoted is one already measured in `ddsp-examples` or the tutorial;
every function name cited exists in `optimizers.lib`. Three references
added: Stearns 1981 and Söderström & Stoica 1982 on the output-error
surface, Hansen 2016 for CMA-ES. `libraries/README.md` mentions the section.

**Gates.** Documentation only; no test parses the overview, and no other
document pointed at the former section 9.
