## `optimizers.lib`: losses that widen the basin (plan phase N5)

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

Last phase of `porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`.
Three losses in the "Losses and Regularizers" section, the library now
importing `fi` and `an`: `corr_loss(a, eps, y, t)`, minus the normalised
correlation of the smoothed signals, scale-free; `bank_log_energy_loss(B,
lo, hi, a, eps, y, t)`, `log_energy_loss` summed over `B` band-pass bands
log-spaced from `lo` to `hi` with the Q set by the spacing, the filter-bank
form of the multi-resolution spectral loss at audio rate;
`frame_spectral_loss(N, eps)`, the FFT magnitude loss of
`ondemand_fad_spectral_loss_008.dsp` promoted to a `2 N`-input block for an
`ondemand` body.

**The gate.** The plan made N5 conditional on a landscape scan of the
string before any learning. `opt_landscape_string.dsp` exposes the pitch
as a control and outputs the four losses at that pitch; a host loop sweeps
it with `faustprobe --set pitch=…` and reads each output's mean. `mse`: the
±1 Hz well on a 0.011 plateau. `corr_loss`: the same ±1 Hz well, the
plateau flat at 0 instead of sloped, a dip at 264 Hz (a harmonic relation).
`bank_log_energy_loss`, 8 bands over 150–4 800 Hz: 0 at 220, 0.5 at ±1 Hz,
0.8 at 222, 1.2 at 224, 1.6 at 226, 2.3 at 228 on one side, 1.1 at 218,
1.8 at 216 on the other, then local extrema at 216 and 232 Hz where the
harmonics of the two strings align; 16 or 32 bands, or a 150–1 200 Hz
range, scale the numbers without widening the slope. So the basin goes from
±1 Hz to about −2/+6 Hz, and the plan's stop condition does not fire.

**Learning through it.** `opt_bank_loss_string.dsp` starts the string at
224 Hz: `descend_1D` on the bank loss with SGD at 1e-4 reaches 220.000 Hz
at 300 000 samples in single precision (the test harness's), 2e-4 does in
double from 218 and 224 Hz, 5e-4 oscillates and Adam wanders, the
tutorial's rule that the optimizer must be slower than the loss's smoothing
(`1 - a = 1e-3`); from 226 Hz single precision still wanders at 400 000.
The disappointing half: `lsq_1D` with NLMS on the waveform error reaches
220 from 224 and 226 Hz as well, carried by the slope of its plateau (it
captures from above up to 228 Hz, the 2026-07 finding), and from 200 or
214 Hz both losses fail, the bank blocked by the harmonic alignments at 214
and 216 Hz. A first draft of the fixture started at 218 Hz claiming the
waveform drifts there; it locks. So the widening is measured and real, and
on this string it buys no start the waveform error cannot handle, which the
fixture, the overview and the plan say.

**Checks.** `opt_loss_symmetry.dsp`: swapping the arguments of `corr_loss`
and of the bank loss changes nothing bit for bit, `corr_loss(t, t)` reads
−0.999996 and the bank loss of a signal with itself is exactly 0.
`opt_bank_loss_tangent.dsp`: `fad` of the bank loss with respect to a gain
against a central finite difference, −43.06843 vs −43.06874, relative gap
7e-6. `opt_frame_spectral_lanes.dsp`: on an 8-sample frame, the loss with
itself is 0 and `loss(2t, t) − loss(0, t)` is 0 bit for bit, the
magnitudes scaling exactly. `opt_all_functions.dsp` now 99 entries, 165
outputs.

**Mutation gate.** The bank's bands spaced linearly instead of
log-spaced (`lo + b (hi - lo) / (B - 1)`, the Q kept): the symmetry and
floor checks still pass, and the landscape scan changes, which is the
plan's reject for this phase: at 222, 224, 226 and 228 Hz the mutated bank
reads 0.67, 0.71, 1.09, 1.30 against 0.81, 1.17, 1.65, 2.27 for the
log-spaced one, the slope between 222 and 224 Hz gone flat. Reverted.

**Docs.** Overview (both languages): section 3 losses row, section 4.8
(the spectral loss no longer pending), three measured rows in section 5,
section 9's loss bullet; the plan carries N5's status and the measured
widths, and with it all six phases of the plan are landed.

## `optimizers.lib`: multi-start and grid-then-gradient (plan phase N2)

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

Fifth phase of `porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`,
a new library section "Multi-Start Loops". `multistart_1D(K, init, loss,
upd, lo, hi, a, reset)` runs `K` loss-first descents in parallel and
outputs the parameter and index of the one whose bias-corrected smoothed
loss is lowest, first on ties (`_argmin(K)`, a fold of triples);
`multistart_lsq_1D` is its least-squares twin, needed because on the string
only the NLMS loop locks from 228 Hz in single precision (N3's finding).
`grid_then_descend_1D(K, T, init, loss, upd, lo, hi, reset)` scores `K`
fixed candidates by their accumulated loss for `T` samples with no tangent,
freezes the best with `init_latch` as the `init` of one `descend_1D` held
until `T` by `init_reset`, and outputs the parameter and the chosen index.
`grid_init(K, lo, hi)` spreads `K` starts at the cell centres of the bounds.

**Fixtures.** `opt_multistart_string.dsp`: four NLMS loops from 176, 200,
228 and 264 Hz; the one from 228 Hz locks, its residual vanishes, the index
is 2 from 16 000 samples on and the pitch `220.000000`; four strings and
their tangents compile in 58 ms (the propagation memo of the morning at
work). `opt_multistart_two_wells.dsp`: four SGD descents from the cells of
`[-3, 3]`, the two from the left end in the deep well and the loop follows
one of them (their losses equal to rounding, the index reads 0 or 1),
`-1.036`. `opt_grid_two_wells.dsp`: eight cells scored for 2 000 samples,
the one at `-1.125` chosen (index 2), the descent from it at `-1.036`; the
held output during the window is the cell minus one engine step, the
loops' reset semantics of N6. The grid is not measured on the string: its
±1 Hz well would need hundreds of cells over the range. A first draft of
`grid_then_descend_1D` passed `best : !, _, !` as a function argument,
which Faust reads as four arguments; parenthesised. `opt_all_functions.dsp`
now 96 entries, 162 outputs.

**Mutation gate.** The fold's comparison inverted (`>` for `<`), so the
selector follows the *highest* loss: the string test fails on its index
assertion. Reverted.

**Docs.** Overview (both languages): section 3 (fifteen sections), a new
4.11, three measured rows in section 5, section 9 (the two forms it used to
announce now exist); the plan carries N2's status and its deviations.

## `optimizers.lib`: restart on a plateau (plan phase N3)

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

Fourth phase of `porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`.
`descend_1D_restart(K, init, loss, upd, lo, hi, W, rel, eps_l, reset)` and
its least-squares twin `lsq_1D_restart` walk a sequence of `K` starting
points `init(k)`: whenever the smoothed loss (bias-corrected, horizon `W`)
is above `eps_l` and not down by `rel` over the last `W` samples, checked
from `2 W` samples after a start, the deviation is zeroed and the next start
is taken. Outputs the parameter and the start index. A sequential
multi-start for the cost of one model. The same test is exposed as
`no_progress(W, rel, eps_l, l)`.

**Why not the gradient.** The plan had the restart driven by `stalled`, a
small gradient under a high loss. Measured with a loss-first Adam loop on
the string, the gradient is *larger* on the plateau (0.025, the loop walking
between 196 and 207 Hz) than in the well (0.003, locked on 220): the plateau
slopes, and during the first 12 000 samples the loss is the same 0.010 on
the drift from 200 Hz and on the descent from 228 Hz. Only the *progress*
of the loss separates them. `stalled` stays for flat plateaus.

**Fixtures.** `opt_no_progress_lanes.dsp` (decaying, constant high,
constant low loss: 0, 1, 0; a first draft without the bias correction read
the decaying segment as no progress, the average starting from zero).
`opt_restart_two_wells.dsp`: `descend_1D_restart` with SGD on the two-well
loss from (1, -1): shallow well (loss 0.29 > `eps_l` 0.1), restart at
8 000, deep well at -1.036 for good. `opt_lsq_restart_two_wells.dsp`:
`lsq_1D_restart` with NLMS on `x · L(p)` against `-0.2 x`, `L` the same
polynomial: the residual vanishes on the slopes of the deep well only
(a first draft put the target at the polynomial's minimum, a double root
where the sensitivity vanishes too and NLMS wobbles); shallow well, restart
at 8 000, a root of `L = -0.2`, residual nil. `opt_all_functions.dsp` now 92
entries, 155 outputs.

**The string bed did not survive.** The plan's fixture was the string
restarted from 228 Hz after its drift from 200 Hz. In double precision
`lsq_1D_restart` with NLMS and a patience of 8 000 did lock (restart at
16 000, 220.000 by 48 000); in single precision it needed 12 000, and with
12 000 double then failed; with a hold of `W` samples after each start (the
loops keep it: the model and its tangent settle on the new parameter while
the engine adapts) the descent from 228 became far slower than a fresh
loop's, and Adam with the hold went to 224 Hz or to 236 Hz and restarted.
The fresh loop's speed is an artifact: NLMS's first steps are huge while its
power estimate is empty, and its tangent starts clean; after a restart the
model, its tangent through the recursion and the engine keep the drift's
state, and the ±1 Hz well does not forgive it. Tuning thresholds on the
fragile bed would have been fitting noise; the library documents the limit
and the loops are measured on the two-well landscapes.

**Mutation gate.** The restart condition forced to 0 in `lsq_1D_restart`:
the index lane stays at 0 and the two-well test fails on its second-start
assertion. Reverted.

**Docs.** Overview (both languages): section 3 rows, section 4.5, five
measured rows in section 5 including the string's failure, section 9; the
plan carries N3's status and its deviations.

## `optimizers.lib`: gradient-free clocked loops (plan phase N1)

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

Third phase of `porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`,
a new library section "Gradient-Free Loops" between the clocked loops and
gating. `spsa_1D_clocked(clock, loss, upd, c, lo, hi, init, reset)` holds a
±1 sign over the frame, evaluates the loss at `p ± c delta` at audio rate on
the same excitation, averages both with `frame_mean`, and on the firing hands
`(L+ - L-) / (2 c delta)` to the ordinary engine and draws the next sign
(an LCG inside the body, hence once per firing; `+1` before the first firing
so the first frame is already a valid estimate). `search_1D_clocked` is the
(1+1) evolution strategy: an incumbent and a candidate `p + sigma u`, the
candidate adopted when its frame loss is strictly lower. `spsa_N_clocked` is
the bus form, one sign vector and still two loss evaluations whatever `N`.
`c` and `sigma` enter the body as inputs, so a ramp on them counts samples.
They learn what `fad` differentiates to zero, for two model copies instead
of a model and its tangent.

**Fixtures and what they measure** (interp, f32, same numbers as
`faustprobe --double`):

- `opt_spsa_vs_fad_gain.dsp`: SPSA and `descend_1D_clocked` on a gain, same
  engine and clock. The symmetric difference of a quadratic loss is its
  derivative, so the two trajectories coincide: difference `0` on every
  sample, `0.700000` at 4 000. This is the independent check of the
  estimator.
- `opt_spsa_int_delay.dsp`: a comb `x + x @ 200` on a first-order low-passed
  noise, the model's delay `de.delay(512, int(d), x)`. The `fad` tangent is
  identically zero (asserted on a lane); SPSA with `c = 2` and Adam 0.5 per
  256-sample frame takes `int(d)` from 160 to 200 by 25 000 samples, holds it
  from 40 000 on, residual 0. The plan's bed was the string; an integer delay
  on its waveform loss sees the ±1 Hz well at one value and a plateau
  everywhere else, so the comb, whose loss is a bowl as wide as the
  excitation's correlation length, replaces it (noted in the plan).
- `opt_search_select2.dsp`: `select2(p > 0.5, 0.2 x, 0.7 x)` against
  `0.7 x`, a step loss. `descend_1D` never moves `p`; the search adopts a
  candidate at 0.83 within the first frames and the frame loss is 0.

Compile time with `faust-rs -time` (release): 27 ms, 28 ms and 11 ms for
the three fixtures against 7 ms for the `fad` clocked gain fixture, the two
model copies and the frame block. `opt_all_functions.dsp` now 89 entries,
150 outputs.

**Mutation gate.** The perturbation sign of one branch flipped so both
copies see `p + c delta`: `g ≡ 0`, the delay never moves and the integer
test fails, the vs-`fad` test too. The acceptance of the search inverted
(`>` for `<`): the search adopts worse candidates and the `select2` test
fails. Both reverted.

**Docs.** Overview (both languages): section 3 (fourteen sections), a new
4.10, three measured rows in section 5, section 9 (what now exists),
references (Spall 1992, Beyer & Schwefel 2002); the plan carries N1's status
and its deviation.

## `optimizers.lib`: `langevin_g`, a noisy engine (plan phase N4)

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

Second phase of `porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`,
one line in the gradient-engine section:
`langevin_g(lr, temp, noise, g) = lr g + sqrt(2 lr temp) noise`, stochastic
gradient Langevin dynamics (Welling & Teh 2011). With `temp` a `ramp_exp`
to zero the walk explores while hot and descends once cold; at `temp = 0`
it is `sgd_g` bit for bit, which the fixture asserts on a third lane.
Documented limit, in the library and in the overview's section 9: noise
leaves a shallow well, it does not pull on a flat plateau, and it does not
choose the deepest well with certainty.

**Fixture** `opt_langevin_two_wells.dsp`: the loss `(p² - 1)² + 0.3 p`, a
shallow well at `0.960` (loss 0.29), a deep one at `-1.036` (loss -0.31), a
barrier of `1.01` at `p = 0.04`; both descents start at `p = 1`, `lr = 0.01`,
temperature `0.5` with a 20 000-sample time constant, unit-variance noise
(`no.noise * sqrt 3`). No data, so the outcome is deterministic given the
noise generator's seed. Measured (200 000 samples, mean of the last
20 000): SGD `0.960`, Langevin `-1.036`, the cold lane identical to SGD.
Rust test `langevin_leaves_the_shallow_well_where_sgd_stays`;
`opt_all_functions.dsp` now 86 entries, 145 outputs.

**Mutation gate.** The `sqrt` dropped from the noise amplitude (`2 lr temp`,
a hundred times smaller at `lr = 0.01`): the walk never crosses the barrier
and the deep-well assertion fails; the identity lane still passes, which is
why the escape check exists. Reverted.

**Docs.** Overview (both languages): section 3 table, section 4.1 origins
row, section 5 measured row, section 9, references; the plan carries N4's
status.

## `fad` compile time, the other half: the recursion arity walks

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

The previous entry's two memos (the evaluator's arity oracle, the result memo
on AD roots) left the same program slow as soon as the estimate reached the
learning loop as a *closure* with no `fad` at all: 4.8 s at `K = 8`, 94 s at
`K = 148`, in the arity phase and again in propagation. Sampled, the time is
under the `Rec` arms of `box_arity_flat_inner` and `propagate_inner`:
`rec_fad_mode` runs two forward-AD reachability scans over both branches
(`contains_forward_ad`, `subtree_consumes_fad_outputs_locally`) and the
`None`/`ExpandAfterRec` modes then ask `box_arity_wiring`, and none of the
three was memoized, although `box_arity_typed` next to them was. On a
hash-consed box DAG each walk costs the number of paths, and every recursion
in the DAG (`si.smooth`, the delay lines of the string) repeats it.

**Fix** (`crates/propagate`). `ArityCache` becomes a struct of four maps
(`typed`, `wiring`, `forward_ad`, `fad_consumed_locally`); `box_arity_wiring`
memoizes through the second, the two scans through the last two and take the
cache as a parameter, from `rec_fad_mode` in both the arity pass and the
engine (and from the output-arity mismatch check). Public API: the type alias
is now a struct with `new()`/`Default`; `docs/code-graphs` regenerated.

**Measured** (release `faust-rs -time`, the hand-written closure loop of the
repro): arity 2.26 s → 0.1 ms and propagation 2.47 s → 2 ms at `K = 8`; the
whole compile 4.8 s → 46 ms, and 94 s → 116 ms at `K = 148`. The shipped
30-lane `opt_init_latch_string.dsp` compiles in 31 ms, its 148-lane variant in
76 ms (10.5 s with the release `faustprobe` of the morning). Generated code
unchanged: propagate, eval, optimizers, DDSP, `rad`/`fad` runtime suites (356
tests) and the golden check pass.

**Test.** `rec_arity_walks_are_linear_on_a_shared_dag`: `(_, x) : + ~ _`
with `x` a 40-level `(x, x) : +` chain over a constant (`2^40` box paths, a
linear signal graph) under a 60 s budget; the first draft composed `x` with
itself, which makes the *signal* graph exponential and timed out for the
wrong reason. Mutation: the `forward_ad` probe removed, the test hangs to its
budget. `porting/MEMOIZATION.md` §2.6 describes the four maps.

**History.** The two compile-time commits were written on a parallel
session's branch that forked before N6; they were rebased onto the N6 commit
so that `main-dev` stays linear, the journal conflict resolved by keeping
both entries. The library's 1D loops keep taking `init` through an input
wire: cleaner, and the compiler no longer needs it.

## `fad` compile time: two memos the seed's expression was paying for

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

Found on the string fixture planned for phase N6 of
`porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`: `op.lsq_1D` on a
waveguide string, with the initial estimate a `K`-lane autocorrelation fold,
compiled with `faustprobe --double` in 34 s at `K = 8` and 115 s at `K = 24`,
about 4 s per lane, against 0.07 s for the same program with `d = estimate`
(no `fad`) and 0.16 s with a latched constant as the initial value. The
seed `p1 = clip(lo, hi, init + prev)` is mentioned a dozen times by
`de.fdelay4` (integer part, fractional part, Lagrange coefficients) and it
carries the whole estimate.

**Where it paid.** `faust-rs -time` with `FAUST_PROPAGATE_PROFILE`: at
`K = 2`, 9.3 s of 9.8 s in propagation, 18 M `seq` calls, the `fad` box
propagated 200 times, `result-memo probes=0`. Two memos were missing, one per
stage.

1. `propagate` (`crates/propagate/src/result_memo.rs`, `engine.rs`). The
   exact Box-to-Signal result memo (§2.15 of `porting/MEMOIZATION.md`) was
   switched off for the whole root of any program containing a `fad` or `rad`
   node, so every shared box subtree was re-propagated once per reference and
   the references multiply along the nesting. The exclusion guarded one side
   effect: under `suppress_fad` (the `ExpandAfterRec` recursion mode) a
   `ForwardAD` box returns its primal bus and appends its seeds to
   `pending_fad_seeds`. That vector is write-only during propagation (only the
   `Rec` arm drains it), so the seeds a call appends are a function of its
   exact key: the entry now stores that delta next to the output bus and a hit
   appends it again. AD roots are eligible; only the clocked wrappers keep the
   root ineligible. Propagation of the repro: 0.03 s at `K = 8`, 0.7 s at
   `K = 148`.
2. `eval` (`crates/eval/src/apply.rs`, `loop_detector.rs`). With propagation
   fixed, evaluation still took 1.8 s at `K = 8` and 36 s at `K = 148`, all of
   it (sampled) in `infer_box_arity`, the evaluator's first-order arity oracle
   that `apply_list` probes for the function and every argument of each
   primitive application. It was an unmemoized recursive walk, so on a shared
   box DAG it cost the number of paths. It is now memoized per `TreeId` in
   `LoopDetector::box_arity_cache` (exact: the arena is hash-consed and
   append-only), with a call-local memo for the detector-less entry point.
   Evaluation of the repro: 12 ms at `K = 8`, 57 ms at `K = 148`.

**Result.** `faustprobe --double … -n 1000` on the repro: 0.09 s at `K = 8`
(was 34 s), 0.4 s at `K = 24` (was 115 s), 2.1 s at `K = 148`, most of it the
JIT. Generated C++ byte-identical at `K = 2`, `4` (against the pre-fix

## Plan: `optimizers.lib` beyond the gradient's basin

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

Follow-up of the non-convexity section: the overview now says what the
library does not offer when the start is in the wrong basin or the parameter
has no derivative. `porting/optimizers-nonconvex-search-plan-2026-09-16-en.md`
plans six additions in the library's idiom, ordered N6 → N4 → N1 → N3 → N2 →
N5 by value, cost and dependency:

- **N6** initialisation and continuation helpers (`init_latch`, `init_pulse`,
  `ramp_lin`, `ramp_exp` with `lr_exp` kept as a bit-identical alias,
  `stalled`), on the state form `init + deviation` where `init` is a signal;
- **N4** `langevin_g`, a noisy engine with an annealed temperature;
- **N1** gradient-free clocked loops, `spsa_1D_clocked`, `search_1D_clocked`
  ((1+1)-ES) and `spsa_N_clocked`: two model evaluations per frame on the same
  excitation, no tangent, so an integer delay, a `select2` or a written table
  become learnable;
- **N3** `descend_1D_restart`, a sequential multi-start driven by a plateau
  detector;
- **N2** `multistart_1D` and `grid_then_descend_1D` on an `argmin_K` fold;
- **N5** basin-widening losses (`corr_loss`, `bank_log_energy_loss`,
  `frame_spectral_loss`), gated by a landscape scan of the string with
  `faustprobe --set` that must show a wider well before any learning.

Each phase names its fixtures, an independent check (SPSA estimate against
the `fad` gradient on a smooth loss; the fold against an argmin computed in
Rust; loss symmetry and floor; ramp alias identity) and a hand-applied
mutation the check must reject. The shared bed is the waveguide string, whose
landscape is the only one the repository has measured (±1 Hz well, drift from
200 and 176 Hz), plus an integer-delay variant on which `fad` gives a zero
tangent. Every number in the plan is a target to measure. Verified while
writing: `an.pitchTracker` exists in the installed faustlibraries; no planned
name collides with `optimizers.lib` 0.8.0. Nothing is implemented.

## `optimizers.lib` overview: a section on non-convexity

Commit date: 2026-09-16

Source commit: this commit

The word "convex" appeared nowhere in `docs/` or `libraries/`, although the
question decides whether a DDSP program learns: gradient descent guarantees
the global minimum only for a convex loss, and almost no loss in the
optimizers documentation is convex (resonator frequency and Q, biquad poles,
notch angle, FDN T60, string delay length, GRU and MLP weights), while nearly
all of them converge. The examples already carried the answer in measured
form: the waveguide string's ±1 Hz well on a flat plateau (228 → 220 Hz
converges, 200 → 190 drifts), the notch basin set by its radius, the
`log_energy_loss` that unsticks a cutoff `mse` leaves at the 20 Hz bound, the
damping annealed from 0.70 to 0.95.

**Docs.** New section 9 in `libraries/optimizers-overview-fr.md` and
`-en.md` (same content, same structure, 16 bold or bulleted leads each),
references renumbered to 10. It states what gradient descent actually asks
of the landscape (a starting point in the right basin, an informative
gradient, comparable scales), sorts the repository's examples into convex
and non-convex, walks through the measured string counter-example, maps
each library tool to one of the three questions (`init`, reparameterisation,
loss choice, continuation, `lm_2D`/`lm_3D`, and the `_rad` direct term that
changes nothing), and lists what the gradient cannot do alone (multi-start,
coarse grid then gradient, gradient-free search, discrete parameters),
saying explicitly that none of it is in the library and sketching the two
natural forms, in-graph and host-side, neither written nor measured. Every
number quoted is one already measured in `ddsp-examples` or the tutorial;
every function name cited exists in `optimizers.lib`. Three references
added: Stearns 1981 and Söderström & Stoica 1982 on the output-error
surface, Hansen 2016 for CMA-ES. `libraries/README.md` mentions the section.

**Gates.** Documentation only; no test parses the overview, and no other
document pointed at the former section 9.
