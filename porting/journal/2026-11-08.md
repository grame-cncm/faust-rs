## fir, codegen: the batch `compute` keeps its state in locals

Commit date: 2026-11-08

Source commit: this commit

The batch contract read and wrote every per-voice state element in its arena at every sample.
Mojo does not keep those in registers, because an arena pointer may alias the outputs, so the
GPU sample loop waited on memory. The plan is
`porting/batch-state-register-promotion-plan-2026-11-08-en.md`, with its outcome in §7.

- `fir::batch::promote_state`, a FIR-to-FIR pass run before `layout_voices`. It promotes every
  `Struct` field that `compute` reaches only as a scalar or at `Int32` literal indices: the field
  is read into a `Stack` local at the head of `compute`, used through it, and written back at
  the end if `compute` stores into it. Delay lines at dynamic indices stay in memory, while
  their counters are promoted. A `Return` or a call to a function of the module disables the
  pass.
- `fir::batch::promote_check::check_promotion`, an independent checker of seven rules. Its core
  rule puts the locals back on their fields and requires the source body exactly, by id after
  importing both modules into one store. The pass returns only checked output (fail closed).
  Each of six producer mutations is rejected by its named rule.
- `MojoOptions::batch_registers`, default on, applies the pass in batch mode;
  `mojo_batch --no-registers` emits the earlier form. Non-batch output is untouched.
- Tests:
  - `crates/fir/src/batch/promote_tests.rs`: promoted and kept fields, entry/body/exit shape,
    composition with `layout_voices`, refusals, the mutations;
  - `crates/compiler/tests/batch_voices_interp.rs`: every program also run promoted, every voice
    bit-identical to its scalar instance at opt 0 and max for 1, 3 and 64 voices, with
    `gpu_modal_fad_voice` added. A structural test checks that the modal voice's sample loop
    reaches no arena once promoted;
  - a Mojo emitter test: no expression of the batch `compute` reads an arena.
- On the GPU:
  - the modal-fit, choir and carillon harnesses validate;
  - the modal epoch matches the text rewrite it replaces (14.8 against 14.7 ms at 1,024 modes,
    67.8 against 67.8 at 4,096, 292 against 293 at 16,384);
  - the carillon at 256 bells went from 0.84 to 0.46 ms per block, ×2.5 over the CPU threads
    where the synthesis records ×1.15.
- `tests/impulse-tests/tools/batch_state_registers.py`, the text rewrite, is removed.
- The `DIFF-BACK-010` batch extension in the compatibility registry is updated.
- Codegen identity, base (`b8ab7d7d`) against new:
  - `-lang mojo -single` and `-double` over every `.dsp` of `tests/` and `examples/`: 1,047
    outputs identical, 241 failing on both sides, no status difference;
  - `mojo_batch --no-registers` against the base `mojo_batch` on the corpus GPU and `fad`
    voices: 19 of 19 identical.
- Gates:
  - fmt, clippy, `cargo test --workspace --all-targets`, the five structure checks and
    `golden-check` pass;
  - `code-graphs` regenerated: 8 new public items, those of the plan's §6;
  - `compile-budget-check` refuses to run on this machine (its calibration takes 3 ms, under
    the 4 ms floor). The pass runs only in batch mode. A manual A/B (`-lang cpp` over the 133
    impulse programs, three runs) timed base and new at 3.0 to 3.6 s alike.

## impulse-tests: modal sound matching by `fad` on the GPU

Commit date: 2026-11-08

Source commit: this commit

A prototype of learning on the batch axis: a bank of N modes fitted to a target by gradient
descent, one GPU instance per mode. The write-up, with every measured number, is the new
"Modal sound matching by `fad`" section of `porting/faust-gpu-audio-synthesis-2026-11-07-en.md`
§5.

- `tests/corpus/gpu_modal_fad_voice.dsp`: one impulse-struck mode (the `fi.nlf2` rotation),
  outputting y and its three tangents by `fad`. faustprobe `--fd-check` in double agrees to
  3e-10. `-pn plain` gives the same mode without tangents.
- `tests/corpus/gpu_modal_fad_group.dsp`: four such modes in one program, the CPU baseline.
  One mode per instance is bound by its recursion's latency (9.1 ns per mode and sample on one
  core); four bring it to 4.7.
- `tests/impulse-tests/archs/modalfadmojogpubatch.mojo`, with modes `validate`, `bench` and
  `fit`:
  - one pass per epoch: the voice kernel reduces its previous block's tangents against that
    block's residual, then computes the next block;
  - a two-stage mix writes the residual;
  - the update is Adam or a damped Gauss-Newton per mode (3×3 Gram matrix, block-Jacobi,
    globalised by undoing steps that raise the loss).
- `validate` checks GPU outputs, residual and gradient sums against the scalar CPU program,
  the closed form and f64 sums: worst 1.1e-3, f32 phase drift.
- `tests/impulse-tests/archs/modalfadcpu.cpp`: the same fit in C++ (clang, `std::thread`), with
  its own minimal `dsp`/`UI` interface and no installed Faust header.
- Makefile targets `mojo-gpu-modal-fad-build` and `mojo-gpu-modal-fad`. They build the GPU
  harness twice, as emitted and with `mojo_batch --no-registers` (next entry), and validate and
  time both.

Results on the Apple M5, one epoch of 1.024 s:

| modes | GPU, state in the arenas | GPU, state in locals | CPU, 10 threads |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,024 | 75 ms | 13.6 ms | 42–49 ms |
| 4,096 | 128 ms | 64 ms | 162–172 ms |
| 16,384 | 659 ms | 291 ms | 622–680 ms |

- With every state access in the voice arenas, the batch code did not beat the CPU below a few
  thousand modes. With the state in locals, first measured with a text rewrite of the generated
  Mojo and now emitted by the compiler (next entry), the GPU gains ×2 to ×3.1.
- Gauss-Newton per mode converges in about 15 epochs; Adam needs about 150.
- The basin of the time-domain loss is about one bandwidth.
- Banks denser than the signal resolves (4,096 and 16,384 modes over 1 s) fit the loss without
  recovering the parameters.

Not done:

- a recorded or rendered target;
- a peak-picking front end;
- `rad`.

Golden refresh: `golden-gen-rust` added exactly `tests/golden/rust/gpu_modal_fad_voice/` and
`tests/golden/rust/gpu_modal_fad_group/`; no C++ snapshot (`fad` is a faust-rs extension).

## fad: the dual memo scoped by binder frame, linear on cascades of twin recursions

Commit date: 2026-11-08

Source commit: this commit

`fdn_online.dsp` of faust-diff-fdn (an `fad` through its octave-band analysis inside
`op.descend_N_fad_clocked`) no longer compiled within the 120 s limit, where its README records
5 s. The compiler was not the change: bisecting faustlibraries points at `37ac4eec`
(2026-10-05), which put a TPT and a direct-form section behind every crossover of
`fi.filterbank`, picked by `select2` on a constant that folds only after propagation. The defect
it exposed is in `ForwardADTransform` (`crates/propagate/src/forward_ad.rs`): the dual memo kept
closed outer entries on entering a recursion body but dropped everything the body added on
leaving it, so the shared input of two sibling recursions was differentiated under each of them
and the work doubled per stage (a three-line reproducer: 0.2 s at 16 stages, 14.4 s at 22).

The memo is now one map per binder frame: an entry goes to the frame of the innermost binder its
term references, keyed by node and depth (closed terms once for the lowering), and leaves with
that binder's frame. The clock-domain renames of `augment_block`, which the value depends on but
the key does not name, now invalidate the entries `needs_rebuild` selects
(`invalidate_twinned_entries`). `fdn_online` compiles in 1.1 s, `_p40` in 3 s (10 s with the
September libraries before the change); the reproducer at 200 stages in 0.7 s. Every `.dsp` under
`tests/`, `examples/` and the faust-diff-* projects (1,063 programs) gives identical C++ or the
identical error, except the seven `fdn_online*` programs that timed out before; with the September
libraries their C++ is identical byte for byte. New test:
`fad_sweep_is_linear_in_a_cascade_of_twin_recursions` (rejecting mutation: the per-body scope,
21,484 → 22,020,076 visits). `porting/MEMOIZATION.md` §2.17 records the rescoping.

## docs: the GPU's smallest audio buffer, and how the player drives PortAudio

Commit date: 2026-11-08

Source commit: this commit

`docs/mojo-gpu-synthesis-en.md` / `-fr.md` gain §8, "Latency: the smallest audio buffer". Measured
with the choir harness (GPU alone): the fixed cost of a block, blocks back to back with 4 singers
(429 partials), is 0.34 ms at 32 and 64 frames, 0.41 ms at 128, 0.55 ms at 256. In real time,
muted, 8 s: 448 singers play without a dropout at 256 frames (median 3.6 ms, 43 ms granted
latency) and at 128 frames (median 1.98 ms, worst 4.9 ms, 30 ms latency); at 64 frames every size
drops out, down to 32 singers (median about 1.0 ms whatever the size, worst 3.0 to 3.4 ms, 23 ms
latency), and at 32 frames down to 8 singers (median 0.57 ms, 20 ms latency). In real time a
64-frame block costs about three times its back-to-back figure: the GPU idles between blocks and
pays the wake-up and the submission again. The section explains the blocking PortAudio loop of the
harness (open with a suggested latency of two blocks, 20 warm-up blocks, per block: controls, GPU
launch and wait, reverb, `Pa_WriteStream` that waits while the buffer is full, underflows counted
from `paOutputUnderflowed`), why the granted latency exceeds the two blocks asked for, and what
could lower it (a callback player fed by a computing thread through a lock-free queue, overlapped
blocks, a GPU kept awake), none written or measured. The limits section and the decision table
now point to it.

## docs: Faust on the GPU, a gentle synthesis; a hall for the GPU choir

Commit date: 2026-11-08

Source commit: this commit

`docs/mojo-gpu-synthesis-en.md` and its French translation `-fr.md`, listed in `docs/README.md`,
explain for a non-specialist what the Mojo backend opens: `-lang mojo` writes Mojo for any Faust
program; thousands of copies of one program can run on a GPU unchanged, the compiler laying their
state out as a structure of arrays; why one program does not speed up (the time order of
recursion, the C++ `-gpu` path at 270 to 2,600 times slower); the measured crossover, the
carillon (and how a fair CPU baseline divided its gain by five), the choir that needs 2.1 times the
whole CPU; and the limits (one shape of problem, latency and blocks, `f32`, divergence, memory, a
hand-written host, Mojo slower than LLVM on the CPU, a young toolchain, the unmeasured
hand-vectorized CPU baseline, one machine), and how CPU and GPU code mix in one Mojo program
(the thousands of copies on the GPU, the single programs such as a reverb on the CPU), with the
commands for both versions of the choir. Same structure as the other gentle syntheses of `docs/`.

The GPU choir gains a second version in a hall, `make mojo-gpu-choir-reverb`, next to the dry
`make mojo-gpu-choir` (both share `mojo-gpu-choir-build`, which builds and validates). `play ...
reverb` passes the stereo mix through the new corpus program `tests/corpus/gpu_choir_reverb.dsp`
(`re.zita_rev1_stereo` mixed with the dry signal) on the CPU, in the playback loop; `dry=`,
`wet=` and `t60=` set it (defaults 0.4, 2 and 5 s: `zita_rev1` returns about 0.29 of its input at
steady state, measured with faustprobe, so the hall sits about 3 dB above the choir). One reverb
is the shape a GPU runs 30 to 100 times slower than a core, so it stays on the CPU: 43 to 53 µs
per block, about 1 % of the budget, and 448 singers still play with no underrun. The version in
the hall also sings a wider choir and a longer score (`Choir(singers, hall)`): registers spread
from C2 to G5, per-part vibrato, breathiness and stage placement (sopranos left to basses right),
harmonics up to 16 kHz so that 448 singers stay at 46,592 partials (median 3.48 ms over a full
64 s cycle), and eight chords (D minor, B flat, F, C, G minor 7, the Neapolitan E flat, A7,
D major), each followed by `gap=` seconds of rest (3 by default) so that the hall's tail is heard.
A rest glides the gains to 1e-9, not 0, to stay out of the f32 subnormal range. The harness's
inner upload loop no longer reuses the outer loop's `k` (it could skip the block of a chord change
in the playback path). Runs made while another choir was playing on the same GPU took 5.5 to
6.1 ms per block and under-ran; alone the choir stays near 3.6 ms. The validation is
unchanged (1.2e-4). Golden refresh: `golden-gen-rust` added exactly
`tests/golden/rust/gpu_choir_reverb/`; no C++ snapshot.

## impulse-tests: an additive choir only the GPU can play; batch interpreter sample rate fixed

Commit date: 2026-11-08

Source commit: this commit

`make mojo-gpu-choir` (`tests/impulse-tests/archs/choirmojogpubatch.mojo`) plays CHOIR singers
(448 by default) in four parts, every harmonic up to 20 kHz one GPU instance of the new corpus
voice `tests/corpus/gpu_choir_partial.dsp`: a sine with its own vibrato, a breath swell and a band
of noise through a TPT state-variable band-pass that follows the vibrato sample by sample (three
sines, a tangent, a noise and a filter per sample). Singers have their own detuning, vibrato,
breath and pan, formant gains of the vowel "a" per part, and sing a four-chord progression. 448
singers are 48,048 partials. The GPU mix of 8 singers equals the same choir of scalar instances on
the CPU to 1.2e-4 relative over 12 s and two chord changes.

Measured (Apple M5, 256 frames, 48 kHz): the best CPU code for the voice is LLVM scalar,
7.4 ns per partial and per sample on one core (`faustbench-llvm`, 256 distinct partials in one
program; `-vec` is slower). Ten copies at once, one per core, reach 1.09e9 partial-samples per
second, a ceiling of about 22,600 partials in real time. The GPU takes 3.61 ms per block for
48,048 partials (68 % of the budget) and 4.93 ms for 68,640 (93 %); the choir of 448 singers needs
2.1 times the whole CPU. In real time, 448 and 576 singers (61,776 partials) play 20 s with no
underrun (medians 3.66 and 4.48 ms; worst blocks 13.6 ms at chord changes, absorbed by the 43 ms
latency). Not measured: a CPU version vectorized by hand across partials, which might come close.
The synthesis gains a section on it, and §1 names it.

The voice glides only `gain`: a one-pole glide of `freq` in f32 stalls up to an ulp over `1 - c`
from its target (2.3e-4 relative, 0.4 cent), at different points on a device that fuses the
multiply-add and one that does not, and the partials' phases drifted apart after each chord
change.

Batch interpreter fix (`crates/codegen/src/backends/interp/mod.rs`): the voice is the first corpus
program that reads the sample rate, and `batch_voices_interp.rs` failed on it. A batch module moves
`fSampleRate` into an arena; the `sample_rate` argument then read a slot the compiler reserved, but
the factory resolved the slot the host writes from `fSamplingFreq`/`fSampleRate` only and reserved
another, so every voice ran at a rate of 1. The factory now falls back to the `sample_rate` slot;
scalar modules always have `fSampleRate` and are unaffected. The differential gains `samplerate`
(rate read in `instanceConstants`, control range above 0 so that voice 0 cannot hide it) and the
choir voice; both fail without the fix. Generated Mojo batch code was never affected. Recorded in
`porting/mojo-gpu-batch-g3-voice-layout-design-2026-11-07-en.md` §9. The compile-budget basket
does not build interpreter factories (C++ codegen and `--check` only), so the fix cannot move it.

Golden refresh: `golden-gen-rust` added exactly `tests/golden/rust/gpu_choir_partial/` and changed
no other snapshot; no C++ snapshot.
