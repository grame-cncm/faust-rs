## optimizers.lib overview: two phases, learning then use

Commit date: 2026-09-07

Source commit: this commit

Section 7 of the overview (EN and FR), from the gated form of a
self-calibrating reverberator: a program that learns in the audio process keeps paying for its
learning once the parameters have settled, `gate_g` and `select2` do not
help since both still compute, and `ondemand` does, a block whose clock is
silent computing nothing. The pattern: the whole loop in an outer
`ondemand` clocked by `1 - done'`, the flag raised by a checkpointed
convergence criterion at a period boundary; a second `ondemand` computing
the filter coefficients when a parameter changes; the measurements (28x
real time learning, 23x gated, 196x stopped, 572x with hoisted
coefficients, 625x for the network with sliders, 631x after a
recompilation with constants) and why the gate is available: the
derivative is a signal of the same program. Scope becomes section 8,
references 9; the tutorial's hygiene section points to it. Documentation
only.

## optimizers.lib overview: a Scope section

Commit date: 2026-09-07

Source commit: this commit

The overview of `optimizers.lib` (EN and FR) gains a section 7, "Scope:
what this reaches, and what it does not", written from the calibration of a
reverberator to measured rooms: what compiler-level differentiation reaches
(calibration and adaptation of parametric models, tens to a few hundred
parameters, inside the audio process or in a host loop, small networks in
Faust, design by objective, a Faust program as a differentiable layer of a
tensor framework as the bridge to build), what it does not reach today and
could (batches and accelerators, graphs beyond tens of thousands of nodes,
a spectral loss without an FFT, the bounds of `rad`: tapes and horizon
equal to the block, no variable delay, no writable table, no clock-domain
crossing, subgradients, no second derivative; the tooling: no execution
graph, no schedules, checkpoints or hyperparameter search, double
precision), and what it will not reach by construction (large-scale deep
learning, dynamic graphs, learning representations from a corpus,
differentiating what is not a signal). References become section 8.
Documentation only.
