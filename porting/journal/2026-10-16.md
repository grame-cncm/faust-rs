## Recursive typing: only the types reading a changed seed leave the memo

Commit date: 2026-10-16

Source commit: this commit (parent `8e0e293`).

With the two fixes below, what remained of the faust-coopiano soundboard's
`fir-prepare-normalize` (15.6 s) was six `retype` passes of 2.6 s, each
spending 0.19 s inferring and the rest in the incremental fixpoint's
bookkeeping (`porting/MEMOIZATION.md` §2.11). `GroupCones` listed for every
group the whole subgraph below its body (31.5 M entries for 2 574 groups,
built in 1.0 s) and each iteration removed the cone of every stale group
from the memo, a node shared by k bodies k times (189 M removals, 1.3 s).
The bank of resonators coupled through the soundboard's output makes most
nodes reach hundreds of groups.

- `crates/sigtype/src/rules.rs`: `SeedWalk` replaces `GroupCones`. It
  records the walk of the bodies' inference once, upside down (for each
  node reached, the nodes visiting it as a child, and each group's
  references). After an iteration that changed seeds, `invalidate` climbs
  from the changed groups' nodes, variables and references: the nodes
  reached are exactly those whose memoised type reads a changed seed; they
  leave the memo, and the bodies among them are the next stale groups (the
  readers the cones gave). Every other type was computed from seeds that
  have not changed since and stays. Approximations, widening ages,
  iteration counts, types and warnings are those of the previous solver;
  mapping status unchanged (adapted fixpoint, C++ Jacobi results).
- Rejected on the way, measured: cones restricted to the nodes reaching a
  seed (`retype` 2.6 → 1.45 s); per-node seed sets with per-group
  dependents lists (27 M entries, worse); one downward walk from every body
  per iteration (`retype` 0.27 s on the soundboard, but N² again on chains
  of recursions: `spectral_level --check` ×1.9 and `parametric_eq` ×1.6 in
  the compile-budget basket; the existing chain test counted body
  inferences only and missed it).
- Tests (`rules::tests`): `a_coupled_bank_gives_the_jacobi_types` (an outer
  recursion summing inner ones that read it and a shared closed
  subexpression, every field and the warnings against the Jacobi
  reference); `invalidation_visits_each_node_once_per_iteration` (6 014
  nodes climbed for a 3 612-node bank over 4 iterations, against
  n·(n + m) = 180 000 per iteration by cones);
  `invalidation_in_a_chain_climbs_only_from_what_changed` (42 378 for 5 646
  body inferences in a chain of 200). Mutations rejected: climbing from
  every node, not evicting or not climbing from references, not evicting

## Isomorphic-group merging opens each group in its own body only

Commit date: 2026-10-16

Source commit: this commit (parent `ad11842`).

`merge_isomorphic_symrec_groups` (`crates/normalize/src/rec_merge.rs`, step
2.8 of `prepare_signals_for_fir_unverified`) is a Rust-only pass, added for
`fad` (plan `porting/normalize-rec-isomorphism-merge-plan-2026-05-01-en.md`).
To compare groups, `open_symrec` rebuilds each body with its own references
replaced by a hole. It did so from an empty cache per group and descended
everywhere, so each group re-interned the whole subgraph below its body,
other groups, tables and controls included: O(R · N), where the plan assumed
O(N + R·D). On the faust-coopiano soundboard that was 169 882 421 node visits
for 2 574 groups, 20 to 27 s of a 34 to 39 s `fir-prepare-normalize`, in a
pass that merges nothing there. Found while measuring the long-sum fix below.

- `FreeVars` (new, private): one walk of the forest per round computes, for
  every node, the sorted set of recursion variables free in it (`{v}` at
  `SYMREF(v)`, minus `v` at `SYMREC(v, _)`, the union of the children
  otherwise; sets shared with a child holding the same one), and how many
  distinct `SYMREC` nodes bind each variable.
- `open_symrec` returns a subtree as is when the group's variable is not free
  in it. Exact when one `SYMREC` alone binds the variable: that node cannot be
  reached from its own body (a child's id is smaller than its parent's), so
  every reachable `SYMREF(var)` is free where it is reached, and a subtree
  without one opens to itself, each `arena.intern` of the full walk meeting an
  existing node (every arena node is interned). Signatures, merges, and the
  nodes created and their order are unchanged, hence SigIds and generated
  code. A variable bound twice keeps the full walk.
- `merge_with` / `open_signatures` (private) carry a `pruned` switch the
  public entry point always sets; the tests compare it with the full walk.
- Treating foreign `SYMREC` bodies as opaque, as the 2026-05-01 plan wrote,
  would be wrong: a nested group may read the enclosing variable. The plan's
  status (it said "Planned") and complexity section are corrected.
- `porting/MEMOIZATION.md` §2.24 records the free-variable table. Mapping
  status: no C++ counterpart (the pass is Rust-only); behaviour unchanged.
- Tests (`rec_merge::tests`):
  - `pruned_opening_matches_the_full_walk`: 300 random forests (groups nested
    in bodies and reading enclosing variables, groups shared between bodies,
    a closed subgraph shared by all, every shape twice under other names so
    that merges happen at every level, a variable rebound by a nested group in
    one forest out of five), built twice: same signatures, same merge, same
    arena length with and without pruning. It rejects four mutations: pruning
    rebound variables too, not recording references, treating foreign bodies
    as opaque, and a binder clearing all free variables.
  - `opening_cost_is_linear_in_nested_and_sibling_groups`: a chain of 200
    groups each reading the previous one and 200 siblings reading a 200-node
    closed subgraph: at most 8 visits per group, against at least N·M = 40 000
    for the full walk; it fails with pruning removed.

## faustprobe: the inline `effect` through the generalized C++ adaptor

Commit date: 2026-10-16

Source commit: this commit (parent `1c43f0c`).

The previous entry chained an inline `effect` of the voices' width at any
width as a faustprobe extension, `getEffectCode`'s adaptor having `adapt`
rules for 1 and 2 channels only. The user chose to generalize the adaptor in
Faust C++ instead and to take the same text here, verbatim:

```
bus(n) = par(i,n,_);
adapt(1,n,m) = bus(n) <: bus(m);
adapt(0,n,m) = bus(n) :> bus(m);
adaptor(F,G) = adapt(outputs(F) <= inputs(G), outputs(F), inputs(G));
```

It splits when the effect has at least as many inputs as a voice has outputs
and merges otherwise, so it keeps the four old cases (their compute code is
byte-identical, in Faust C++ and in faust-rs) and accepts every pair where one
count is a multiple of the other; 3 -> 2 is refused, as before. Of the
variants weighed, a `route` with cyclic pairs accepted every pair but mixed
3 -> 2 arbitrarily, and was rejected for that.

- **Faust C++**: commit `59b373452` on branch `effect-adaptor-general`, cut
  from `origin/master-dev` (`1a52f7785`, 2.90.8) in the worktree
  `../faust-effect-adaptor`, not pushed: `poly-dsp.h` and the 24 `faust2*`
  scripts of `-effect auto`. Checked: each script's adaptor text on 1 -> 2,
  2 -> 1, 5 -> 2 and 3 -> 2; the interpreter poly factory on the same programs
  (5 -> 2: 5 outputs and no effect with the old header, 2 outputs through the
  effect with the new one); `faust2caconsole -nvoices 4 -effect auto` on a
  five-channel instrument, which failed (`pattern matching failed ...
  matches argument list (5)`) and now builds. The pinned reference
  `8eebea429` does not have it yet.
- **faust-rs**: `PolyProbe::try_extract_inline_effect` compiles that wrapper
  once. Only when it fails is the effect compiled alone, to tell an
  instrument without `effect` from one the adaptor refuses, which keeps its
  warning (now "one count must be a multiple of the other"). The extension
  is removed from DIFF-CLI-011.

Tests: `tests/poly_probe.rs` adds the adaptor's 4 -> 2 merge (channel i into
input i mod 2) and checks the 1 -> 2 split's samples; snapshot
`poly_effect_mismatch` takes the new warning. Docs: user guide §10, the
faustprobe skill.

## faustprobe `--nvoices`: an effect that changes the channel count, and `--out`

Commit date: 2026-10-16

Source commit: this commit (parent `50e3267`).

Found while porting the coopiano piano to Faust (sibling project
`faust-coopiano`, report `faust-rs-issues/RAPPORT.md` §5): voices of five
channels (the soundboard regions) into a body that makes them stereo could not
be rendered under `faustprobe --nvoices`, and the project had to render the
voices to a five-channel file, feed the body separately, and keep a native
renderer.

- **Panic.** `PolyProbe::assemble` set `outputs` to the voices' outputs, which
  sized the statistics, the frame buffer, the CSV header and the
  `--list-params` line, while `compute_with_inputs` returned the effect's: a
  2-channel voice into `effect = + : *(2.0)` indexed past the single output
  (`index out of bounds: the len is 1 but the index is 1`). `PolyProbe` now
  keeps `bus_outputs` (one voice's, the width of the mix bus, which
  `poly::mix_check_voice` requires equal to the voices') and `outputs` (the
  effect's when there is one), with a public `bus_outputs()`.
  `--list-params` says `effect: yes (2 -> 1)`.
- **Inline effect extraction.** `getEffectCode`'s adaptor has `adapt` rules
  for 1 and 2 channels only, so the C++ wrapper finds no effect behind a
  five-channel voice (`adapt(5,5)` matches no rule), and the Rust port then
  dropped the effect silently. The extraction now compiles the effect alone
  (`process = dsp_code.effect`) and keeps it when its inputs are the voices'
  width, whatever it is (for 1 and 2 this is what the adaptor's identity
  rules produce), and only otherwise compiles the C++ wrapper verbatim for the
  1 <-> 2 adaptations. An inline `effect` that compiles and that neither
  reconciles is left out with a warning (`PolyProbe::warnings()`, printed on
  stderr as `warning: ...`), no longer in silence. Chaining past two channels
  is a faustprobe extension, recorded in DIFF-CLI-011 of the differences
  registry. An explicit `--effect FILE` of the wrong width stays an error.
- **`--out` under `--nvoices`.** The refusal is lifted: the writer is created
  before anything is printed with the instrument's outputs, every frame of the
  window is pushed, and the statistics go to stdout, as on the scalar path;
  `--every` is refused with the scalar path's wording. The CSV dump and the
  `.npy` of a chord render agree bit for bit.

Tests: `tests/poly_probe.rs` (an effect that mixes 2 -> 1 gives the render,
its statistics and `compute` one channel; inline effects of 3 -> 2, the
adaptor's 1 -> 2, a 3-into-2 mismatch with its warning, and no effect at
all); output snapshots `poly_effect_*` (programs `poly_mixdown`, `poly_widen`,
`poly_body`, `poly_mismatch` in `tests/output/dsp/`) and `poly_out_*`
(`.npy`, JSON, raw `.f64` of a mixed-down instrument, `--every` refused),
replacing `poly_refuses_out`; `poly_list` now ends `effect: yes (1 -> 1)`.
Docs: user guide §6 and §10, the faustprobe skill.

Checked on the instrument itself: `faustprobe --double --nvoices 4 -I dsp
--chord 60,64,67:90@0..22050 -n 44100 --out x.npy dsp/coopiano.dsp` renders
`(44100, 2)`, finite (peak 0.311 / 0.284), bit-identical to the same voices
with `--effect dsp/coopiano_body.dsp`; r10b renders one channel. The compile
takes 122 s and 11.8 GB of peak RSS, the evaluation cost of report §1-§2, not
this change. `docs/code-graphs/public-api-index.md` also picks up line
numbers that earlier commits left stale. The compile-budget gate does not
apply: nothing of the compilation pipeline is touched.

## Long sums: greatest_divisor scans only the pairs that can share a divisor

Commit date: 2026-10-16

Source commit: this commit (parent `3e1ddd5`).

`:>` builds the left-nested sum `((x0 + x1) + x2) + …`, and `simplify`
normalizes every partial sum (`normalize_add_term`). At each level,
`Aterm::greatest_divisor` evaluated `gcd` and `complexity` on every pair of
the `k` terms, so `Σ k²/2 ≈ n³/6` pair evaluations for a sum of `n` terms. The
C++ reference has the same loop (`aterm.cpp` `greatestDivisor`). The
faust-coopiano port hit it: two 732-term sums of its soundboard had to be
grouped by 32 behind an opaque `max(x, -1e300)` (faust-coopiano
`faust-rs-issues/RAPPORT.md`, point 3, reproducer `r02_normalize_long_sum.sh`).

- `crates/normalize/src/aterm.rs`: `greatest_divisor` scans only the pairs
  `DivisorPairs` reports. A gcd has a non-zero complexity only when the two
  terms share a factor with same-sign exponents or a coefficient magnitude
  other than 1; an inverted index (factor, exponent sign) → term positions and
  buckets by coefficient magnitude list those pairs, a superset of the
  non-trivial ones. Pairs are visited in the order of the full scan (`i`
  ascending, partners ascending), so the first strictly greater complexity
  still wins ties. The index holds positions only and the gcds evaluated are
  the same: no node is created, SigIds and generated code are unchanged. The
  term list is borrowed instead of cloned, and an aterm of one term (every
  product simplify normalizes as an add-term) returns before any index is
  built. Not a memo: the index is built and dropped per call, so
  `porting/MEMOIZATION.md` is unchanged. Mapping status `1:1` (same result as
  C++ `greatestDivisor`, fewer pairs evaluated).
- Tests (`aterm::tests`):
  - `greatest_divisor_matches_the_full_pair_scan`: 2000 random aterms
    (factors of three orders, exponents −2..2 with 0 kept, coefficients
    mixing ±1, 0, ±0.0, equal magnitudes and Int/Real twins) against the full
    scan kept as test oracle; same coefficient SigId, same factors, no node
    created. It rejects three mutations: dropping the magnitude buckets,
    visiting partners in descending order (tie-break), and leaving magnitude 0
    out of the buckets.
  - `long_sum_normalization_evaluates_no_trivial_gcd`: simplify of a 512-term
    `:>` chain (terms `x`, `c·x` with distinct `c`, `-x`) evaluates 0 gcds
    (a thread-local counter, `cfg(test)` only) and keeps the 512 terms; with
    the full scan restored it fails (millions of evaluations).
  - `long_sum_with_a_shared_coefficient_stays_linear`: `0.5·x0 + 0.5·x1 + …`
    factorizes at every level and stays within `2n` evaluations.

Measurements (release, this Mac, `fir-prepare-normalize` in seconds,
`r02_normalize_long_sum.sh`):

| n | one sum, before | one sum, after | grouped by 32, before / after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 128 | 0.025 | 0.017 | 0.0073 / 0.0074 |
| 256 | 0.062 | 0.023 | 0.0120 / 0.0118 |
| 512 | 0.377 | 0.071 | 0.0241 / 0.0233 |
| 1024 | 2.859 | 0.249 | 0.0492 / 0.0476 |

The single sum goes from cubic to quadratic (×3.5 per doubling, 3.9 s at
n = 4096), not linear: every level still re-flattens its `k` terms
(`Aterm::from_sig`), indexes them and rebuilds the sum (`normalized_tree`),
O(k) each, as C++ does. Making it linear needs an incremental aterm per partial
sum (option C of the report), left for later.

The faust-coopiano soundboard (`dsp/coopiano_body.dsp -double`), whose
generated C++ is byte-identical before and after, with `gsum` replaced by a
plain `:>` (ungrouped) and as shipped (grouped):

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| ungrouped, `fir-prepare-normalize` | 39.4 s | 37.3 s |
| ungrouped, simplify #1 + #2 | 1.28 + 2.39 s | 0.27 + 0.41 s |
| grouped, `fir-prepare-normalize` | 34.9 s | 38.6 s (noise) |

The long sums are no longer what that phase costs on the body: per-pass
timings (temporary instrumentation, not committed) give
`merge_isomorphic_symrec_groups` 20.5–27.4 s from run to run although it

rebuilds every recursion's body with a fresh cache per recursion, so nested
recursions are re-opened once per enclosing group. That is the next target

Option B, skipping retype #3 and simplify #2 when

`ondemand_fft_framed_004.dsp`, operand order in `ctl_03_smoothing.dsp`, field
and table order in `dx7_alg5.dsp`.

Validation: the corpus differential (base vs this change, every `.dsp` under

finds no difference in generated code, exit status or diagnostics: 595

## eval: pattern matchers are persistent values (`DIFF-GAP-018` closed)

Commit date: 2026-10-16

Source commit: this commit (parent `d854350`).

A `case` function passed as an argument and applied more than once to
different arguments kept the pattern-variable bindings of its first
application: `h(p) = p(2), p(3); process = h(case { (x) => x + 1; });` failed
with `FRS-EVAL-0099` (C++: `3, 4`), and with a later rule binding no
variable, `f = case { (x) => x + 1; (3) => 100; }; process = h(f), f(3);`
silently gave `3, 100, 4` (C++: `3, 4, 4`). `apply_pattern_matcher` bound
the variables in place into the matcher's rule environments, which every
copy of the matcher shares (a `boxPatternMatcher` handle, a matcher bound to
a parameter); C++ pushes a new layer per binding

## eval: port of the C++ application memo (`gApplyMemo`)

Commit date: 2026-10-16

Source commit: this commit (parent `bf65d31`).

A call inside the body of a lambda was evaluated again at every application of
the lambda, so `f_i(m) = f_{i-1}(m) : \(x).(x + 0.5 * f_{i-1}(m))` cost `2^D`,
and a `case` recursion with overlapping calls (`fib`, `ma.chebychev`) grew like
Fibonacci. Both were found porting the coopiano piano to Faust
(`faust-coopiano/faust-rs-issues/RAPPORT.md`, items 1, 2 and 13), whose voice
spent 12.8 s and 11 GB of resident memory in evaluation. The cause: every
application evaluates its body in a new environment layer, which the
`(expr, env)` eval cache has never seen. Upstream Faust fixed the same cost
with `gApplyMemo` (`6789c5da8`, #1290), absent from the reference branch
`8eebea429`.

- `crates/eval/src/apply.rs`: `beta_reduce` memoizes one beta-reduction of an
  abstraction closure, keyed by the `abstr`, the identity of the captured
  environment and the evaluated argument; the head of
  `apply_pattern_matcher_value` memoizes a fresh matcher applied to its full
  argument list, keyed by the `case`, the environment it was evaluated in and
  the arguments. Only successful box and closure results are stored. Mapping
  `adapted` (C++ keys on the hash-consed closure or `boxPatternMatcher` tree and
  the whole argument list): rationale, the treatment of box functions and the
  lifetime are in `porting/MEMOIZATION.md` §2.4e.
- A matcher is fresh (`PatternMatcherValue::fresh_env`) until it is stored as a
  `boxPatternMatcher` handle or read from an environment. A shared matcher
  keeps the bindings of its earlier applications, because pattern variables
  are bound in place into its rule environments; that defect predates the memo
  and is recorded as `DIFF-GAP-018`; the memo is kept off shared matchers so it
  does not spread it.
- `LoopDetector::pinned_environments` keeps alive every environment store an
  apply-memo key points to, so its address cannot be reused within the pass.
- The automaton cache holds `Rc<Automaton>`, shared with every matcher,
  instead of a deep copy per `case` evaluation, lookup and handle read.
- `FAUST_EVAL_APPLY_PROFILE=1` prints the memo's probes and hits per kind and
  the sizes of the memo, of the eval cache and of the environment store.
- Measured (release, Apple M5): `r01_eval_call_reuse.sh` lambda column 67 ms
  to 0.10 ms at `D = 14`; `fib(22)` 194 ms to 0.19 ms; the coopiano voice
  evaluation 12.8 s to 2.0 s, maximum resident set 11.1 GB to 4.5 GB, whole
  compilation 22.9 s to 12.1 s, generated C++ byte-identical. Three
  compilations of the voice at once now take 14.8 s each with 0.6 s of system
  time (`faust-rs` to C++; the report measured 216 s each and 126 s of system
  time through `faustprobe` before). The rest of the
  voice's evaluation is the eval cache itself, 10 M entries of about
  128 bytes.
- Documentation corrected: `MEMOIZATION.md` §2.4c and §3.7 said C++
  hash-conses its environments. Its layers are `unique` (`pushNewLayer`); its
  cross-level hits come from `evalIdDef`, which evaluates a bound value under
  the empty environment, and from `gApplyMemo`. §3.7 is reframed as bound
  values evaluated under a canonical environment.
- Tests: four in `crates/eval/src/apply.rs`, each under a 60 s budget, with
  five mutations rejected (memo off for abstractions or for matchers, the
  environment dropped from either key, a stored handle kept fresh).
- Compile-budget basket, release binaries before and after, best of five: no
  case slower beyond noise (worst 1.03 on an 8 ms case); `dx7_alg5` `--check`
  124 ms to 76 ms, `reverb_designer` 186 ms to 136 ms. The
  `compile-budget-check` gate itself refuses on this machine (calibration
  `karplus.dsp` 3 ms, under its 4 ms floor), as it did before this change; the
  comparison was done by hand for that reason.
- Validation: fmt, clippy `-D warnings`, the workspace tests (3219 passed),
  the five structure gates and `golden-check` (292 cases) pass.
