# 2026-09-10

## BRA: block constants are not taped, delays with a non-literal amount, a 64-bit dsp layout

Commit date: 2026-09-10

Source commit: this commit

Three limits met by a 16-line differentiable FDN (the DAFx26 model of
Ibnyahya & Reiss: 170 RBJ biquad sections, 279 parameters, 48 kHz, one
`rad` over the whole response), each reproduced on a few lines.

**Taped block constants.** `is_trivially_reverse_evaluable` had no arm for
`Min`/`Max`/`Pow`/`Atan2`/`Fmod`/`Remainder`/`Select2`, and `ma.SR` is
`min(192000, max(1, fconstant(...)))`: every coefficient computed from the
sample rate was "not trivially re-evaluable" and taped per sample, with its
sub-expressions, 21 tapes for one RBJ section instead of 5 (`one_peq.dsp`
against `fi.tf2` with slider coefficients). The arms are added, the
operands being stateless; and `ensure_bra_tape_stores` keeps one tape per
lowered FIR value (`tape_by_value`): the recursion slot read inside a body
through `SYMREF` and outside it through `SYMREC` lowered to the same load
and got two tapes. The FDN goes from 4449 to 1391 tapes and from 3.2 to
1.0 s per epoch of 65 536 frames (`bra_tapes.rs`: 2 tapes for a one-pole
with a coefficient through the clamped sample rate, 2 for a slot read on
both sides of its recursion).

**Delays with a non-literal amount.** `propagate_bra_delay_adj` read the
amount with `tree_to_int(..).unwrap_or(0)`: a slider-driven or time-varying
amount was a zero delay, the identity, and the gradients through a
recursion holding such a delay were silently wrong (`y = x + g fdelay(d,
y)`: `rad` −1.08 for `d` against −0.0003 by finite differences, 1.08
against 1.59 for `g`). New `propagate_bra_variable_delay_adj`: the adjoint
is scattered to `n - d[n]` through an `S = D + 1` slot buffer (`D` the
amount's bound from `delay_size_for_amount`, the one sizing the forward
line), read and cleared at slot `n % S`; `d[n] == 0` lands at the same
step, a target before the block is dropped, and the amount is one of the
integers the sweep replays (`collect_delay_amounts`, taped like a
`select2` condition when it varies). A non-literal amount read directly
on a recursion output is rejected in the pre-scan rather than
approximated; `~` never produces it. `rad_runtime.rs`: a slider amount in
a loop, a time-varying amount in a loop, a fractional amount as a seed in
feed-forward, all against finite differences.

**The forward read of such a delay.** Found by the same finite
differences: `lower_fixed_delay`'s `ExactShift` arm read the recursion's
shift array at `total_delay`, the sizing bound, whatever the runtime
amount, so `0.5 : + ~ (@(int(d)) : *(g))` stayed at 0.5 for ever (the
`Circular` arm already used the runtime amount). The arm reads
`amount + implicit_delay` for a non-literal amount
(`recursion_delay_amount.rs`, against the literal program and against
the recurrence).

**A 64-bit `dsp*` layout.** The Cranelift struct offsets were `u32`:
4449 tapes of 131 072 doubles overflowed ("Cranelift dsp* layout size
overflow"), a block of 1.37 s at 48 kHz at most. `StructFieldLayout` and
`StructLayoutPlan` carry `u64` offsets and sizes, added to the base
pointer as 64-bit immediates (`field_offset_imm`); `align_up` takes a
`u64`. The FDN runs on a block of 345 600 frames (7.2 s, `-bra-tape
524288`), 5.8 GB of tapes, 8 s per epoch. `tests.rs`: a 4 GiB array
followed by a scalar.

Documented in `docs/rad-note-en.md` §4 and `docs/rad-usage-en.md`.

## Evaluator: trees in normal form are not re-evaluated

Commit date: 2026-09-10

Source commit: this commit

`ba.take` over a list of 1090 coefficients (the differentiable FDN of the
DAFx26 paper, its coefficients as one list in the manner of `fdn.lib`) did not
finish; the scaling was cubic, 0.7 s at 120 elements, 4 s at 240, more than
two minutes at 480, where the C++ compiler takes 7 s at 1090. The sample showed
`eval_value → map_children → eval_value → …` a thousand frames deep: in
`take(n, (x, xs)) = take(n - 1, xs)` the variable `xs` is bound to the tail of
the list, an already evaluated tree, and `eval_ident_value` re-evaluates the
bound tree in the current environment; every recursion level is a fresh layer
the `(expr, env)` cache has never seen, so the tail is walked whole at every
level, `O(n)` per level, `O(n²)` per `take`, `O(n³)` in all. The C++ compiler
pays the same walk once per `(tree, env)`, but its layers are hash-consed, so
the calls of different `take`s share environments and cache entries.

New `eval/src/normal_form.rs`: a tree the evaluator has already produced
(`LoopDetector::evaluated_boxes`) and built only of numbers, wires, cuts,
primitives, waveforms, widgets whose label carries no `%` variable and whose
parameters are in normal form, and the compositions of such trees, evaluates
to itself in every environment; `eval_value` returns it without walking it and
without a cache entry per layer. The verdict is memoized per tree
(`normal_form_cache`), the walk is iterative (a list of a thousand elements is
a tree a thousand deep). Excluded: identifiers, applications, abstractions,
closures, `case`, `with`, `letrec`, iterations, accesses, components, metadata,
routes, foreign declarations, clock-domain blocks, the AD primitives, unknown
nodes, i.e. everything evaluation can rewrite; a widget label with an
unresolved `%name`; and a `:` whose left side is a numerical tuple. The last
two exclusions came from the tests: source trees must still meet the nesting
budget and fold their constants, so only produced trees take the fast path;
and a produced tree is not always a fixed point either: the application of a
primitive to constants gives `(20 : log)` unfolded and counts on its next
evaluation to fold it to a number, which the seed of a `fad` in
`descend_1D` relied on (the tutorial's §5.2 and §7.2 stopped learning: the
seed and its occurrence no longer the same tree). Result: 480 elements in
1.0 s, 1090 in 3.1 s (the C++ compiler: 6.9 s), the remaining cost the
quadratic pattern-matching itself; the expansions and signal graphs of the
corpus are unchanged (goldens, `expand_corpus`), the tutorial's programs learn
as before. `eval_normal_form.rs`: 480 elements, the sum of the list checked.
